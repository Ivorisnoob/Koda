# Music playback (`service/MusicService.kt`, `CrossfadeEngine`, AutoMix)

Reference detail moved out of `CLAUDE.md` so it loads only when needed. `CLAUDE.md` keeps the summary, the invariants and the silent-failure index; this file keeps the reasoning and the scars. Markers (`[verified]`, `[scar]`, `[judgement]`, `[drifts]`) mean what `CLAUDE.md` says they mean. If a change contradicts a sentence here, fix the sentence in the same commit.

Two entirely separate pipelines. **Music**: `MusicService` (Media3 `MediaLibraryService`) plus `PlayerViewModel` over a `MediaController` - background playback, notifications, queue. **Video**: `VideoPlayerViewModel` owns its own `ExoPlayer` directly - DASH or merged progressive, PiP in `VideoPlayerOverlay`. `service/VideoPlaybackService.kt` only borrows that player to publish a media session (lock screen, notification, headset buttons, foreground status); it never owns or releases it.

**Music modes are service-owned and durable.** Shuffle uses one persisted seed and the same explicit `ShuffleOrder` on both crossfade players, so swapping players cannot silently generate a new order and replay songs. Shuffle, repeat and an active sleep timer survive process recreation; end-of-track sleep applies to both players while duration sleep stores a wall-clock deadline. Sessions checkpoint every 15 seconds and on backgrounding. Restoring a music placeholder must preserve its saved seek when the real stream replaces it. Video sessions restore queue, item and position but deliberately exclude live and completed videos.

**Replacing a music stream is not a request to restart it.** [scar] Error recovery replaced the current source and called `play()` without restoring its position, so a recoverable stream failure could replay the song from zero. `replaceMusicSource` retains the current queue index, position and playback intent at apply time, seeks before preparing, and leaves future-item replacements alone. Prefetch uses it too: an upcoming item can become current while resolution is suspended. Validation and prefetch apply only to a still-placeholder occurrence, so two awaiters of the same resolution cannot reload an already-resolved source. Keep the occurrence checks around these calls; preserving a position does not make a stale queue result safe. This fixes a code-confirmed restart path; the error triggering the reported AutoMix restarts has not been reproduced on a device.

**A failed AutoMix silence probe is unknown audio, not silence.** [scar] The sparse tail scan broke out on an empty or failed decode, then returned its full sixty-second skip allowance. A ninety-second song could therefore become eligible for a transition at thirty seconds. `probeTrailingSilenceMs` returns zero on an unreadable sample, and audio-profile version 7 invalidates the old cached measurements. Preserve the distinction between a successfully decoded silent sample and no sample at all.

**AutoMix decisions must earn their processing.** [judgement] `TransitionPlanner` uses short unprocessed fades for incompatible or unknown tempo pairs; long overlaps need reliable matching pulses, and four-beat alignment additionally needs independent bar-accent confidence. Harmonic evidence gates the outgoing filter, relative major/minor keys are compatible, and short tracks bound their own overlap. Consecutive numbered tracks from the same album/artist/disc bypass AutoMix when unshuffled. [scar] A quiet final half-second had also authorized a scan over the preceding twenty seconds, even when those seconds contained music. Only a wholly silent, complete tail can extend its silence estimate beyond the decoded window. Version 7 profiles require complete head/tail measurements, use the source duration and check a common cancellation/deadline budget between blocking operations. `clen` supplies total size when the prefetch cache has not recorded it yet.

**The user's playback speed is the engine's definition of "normal", not 1.0.** [scar] Every resting tempo write in `CrossfadeEngine` was a literal `PlaybackParameters.DEFAULT`, and AutoMix's own correction is a +/-4% ratio around the source tempo, so a listener who had chosen another speed would have been snapped back to the recorded one by the next transition, mid-song. `setBaseSpeed` is that baseline: transition speeds multiply it, `releaseTempo` eases back to it, and a speed changed during an overlap abandons the overlap the way a shuffle or repeat change does, since its cue and the clock speeds `runFade` captured were planned against the tempo that just moved. `TransitionPlanner` takes it too, because `incomingCue` turns a distance on the source timeline into a wall-clock delay. **The floor is 0.1x and it is Media3's, not a taste call** [verified September 2026 against the 1.11.0 bytecode]: `DefaultAudioSink` runs `PlaybackParameters` through `Util.constrainValue(0.1f, 8f)`, so a smaller rate is clamped at the sink while the player goes on reporting what it was handed - the audio would run at 10% while the position clock extrapolated at 5%. The rate lives in `MusicService` rather than on a player, since two engines alternate tracks, and reaches the UI through session extras; `setSessionExtras` **replaces** the bundle rather than merging, so the sleep timer and the speed publish through one `publishSessionState`.

**A crossfade is invalidated by time and target changes as well as item changes.** [scar] A same-item seek emits no media-item transition, so it previously left a fade running against the old clock. The service cancels on seeks and mode changes; the engine validates the target occurrence and natural successor throughout preparation and mixing and treats reported-clock movement as estimation error to be absorbed rather than a reason to abort, up to a budget: a normalized clock *step* above 150ms between ticks re-anchors the drift baseline, and only **net** re-anchored displacement past 1100ms - just above the 1s discrepancy ceiling Media3 will smooth at all (`MAX_POSITION_DRIFT_FOR_SMOOTHING_US`), which bounds every sanctioned correction - abandons the overlap, as does residual gradual divergence past the same bound. Net, never a sum of absolute steps: reads also spike ~250ms off the trend and return within a tick or two in alternating-sign pairs [measured September 2026, emulator], so summing the churn exhausted any budget while nothing had moved - a spike and its return cancel, where a real discontinuity displaces the clock and never comes back. And tolerating the lag has a consequence the old tight cap was masking: the curve advances on the slower of the two clocks while the fade length was planned on the outgoing one, so the outgoing track can now run out before t reaches 1 - and it still holds the full queue, so Media3 advances it itself, restarting at full volume the song the standby had spent the whole fade bringing in. The outgoing entering its END_GUARD_MS window therefore *completes* the swap rather than racing that advance. [scar] That shape was reached in two wrong steps, each verified against the layer it misread. A flat 150ms absolute cap abandoned **every** overlap on ordinary hardware: the incoming player's AudioTrack is always fresh, and Media3's sink slews onto the AudioTimestamp clock at up to 10% of elapsed time per read [verified September 2026 against the 1.11.0 AudioTrackPositionTracker bytecode], so the reported clock legitimately drifts by the device's output latency - 200ms+ is ordinary, Bluetooth more - during the first seconds of every fade while the audio itself is aligned; fades shorter than ~1.5s completed before the correction accrued, which made the breakage look intermittent instead of total. Then an abort-on-step version assumed that slew limit holds where the fade reads: it does not - the sink smoothing runs on the playback thread, while `player.currentPosition` on the app thread extrapolates between internal anchors and re-anchors in single reads, so the same settle arrives as one step of roughly the output latency [measured September 2026, emulator: 249-280ms at every fade start, abandoning every overlap again]. Audible discontinuities do not need this check to be caught: the service cancels on seeks, the occurrence guards catch queue changes, and STATE_IDLE catches errors. One failed automatic pair falls back to normal advancement instead of reopening the standby source every monitor tick. Session handoff failure restores the active player and listener; once handoff succeeds, retiring the old engine cannot clear the new one. The planner, rhythm estimator and simulated two-player tests exercise these rules without claiming device/audio-sink validation.

**A transition in flight owns queue indices, and replacing the queue invalidates every one of them.** [scar] A crossfade, and a manual skip still resolving its target, are both plans made against the queue as it stood when they started - `targetIndex`, `pendingTargetIndex`, the splice in `completeSwap`. Tapping a song in a playlist replaces that queue, and nothing dropped the plan. `completeSwap` guarded only by comparing the media id *at its target index*, which is no guard at all in the case that actually happens: reopening the playlist you are listening to puts the same song back at the same index, so the check passed and the session was handed to the standby player carrying the old queue's next track - the tapped song never played and a neighbour did. The same replacement left a running fade measuring positions of a track no longer playing, so its curve never advanced and `isFading` stayed true, which blocks the next real crossfade and leaves `pendingTargetIndex` addressing a queue that is gone, so the following Next or Previous jumps somewhere arbitrary; and the old `fallbackManualJump` (since removed) detected the moved queue and then seeked to the bare index anyway. **Anchor on the queue occurrence, never on the index or media id alone**: duplicate playlist entries and a rebuilt copy of the same queue legitimately reuse a media id, while `MusicQueueItem.id` identifies the exact occurrence. The fade carries that id, checks it before touching clocks or volume, and abandons when the outgoing player leaves it; `onMediaItemTransition` drops any overlap whatever the reason (the engine's own swap never reaches that callback, so arriving there mid-fade always means something else moved); and a degraded jump verifies the intended occurrence is still at its index before seeking. The validation, prefetch and delayed error-retry paths obey the same rule: a stale result cannot replace or skip a newly tapped occurrence, and because resolution is single-flight by track id, its URI is rebound to each caller's `MediaItem` rather than copying the winning caller's occurrence id into duplicate rows. Cancelling an overlap cleans the standby synchronously; the cancelled fade coroutine must not clean it a second time later, because the standby may already belong to a new request. Queue replacement is one atomic `setMediaItems` call rather than a visible sequence of set/add mutations. Anything new that plans against a queue index across a suspension point needs the same occurrence anchor.

**Occurrence ownership extends beyond the overlap coroutine.** [scar] The September 2026 JVM audit reproduced stale manual jumps when only the outgoing row changed, suppressed prefetch for duplicate/rebuilt rows, a 403 retry resetting a new queue after its visitor refresh, and delayed gain/tempo writes on reused players. Manual requests retain both occurrences and their player; current-row taps and controller seek commands cancel even when Media3 emits no discontinuity; pending indices validate on read, before the next fade tick. Prefetch admission is occurrence-scoped while extraction and byte caches remain song-scoped. External controller rows acquire an occurrence id on ingress, preserving existing metadata extras. Post-swap tempo release cannot compete with a new overlap. Platform playback resumption now verifies the same empty player before and after IO: Media3 1.11's successful resumption callback applies its returned queue without rechecking. The regression cases for replacement, retaps, duplicates and seeks live in `service/MusicServiceOccurrenceTest.kt` and `service/CrossfadeEngineTest.kt`; read those before changing any of this, because each one names the failure it was written against. Keep shared song caches independent of queue ownership; only a still-valid occurrence may apply their results to playback.

**A notification permission check is not permission to post later.** [scar] Android can revoke `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` between `checkSelfPermission` / `areNotificationsEnabled()` and the binder call. Download completion/failure, download foreground updates and the optional playback Live Update all catch `SecurityException` at `notify()`; a cosmetic notification must never crash playback or the transfer service. The initial foreground promotion is separate and already catches platform refusal, because a service started with `startForegroundService` must stop promptly if promotion fails.

**Single-button wired headsets are resolved in `MusicService`, and only `KEYCODE_HEADSETHOOK` is intercepted.** [verified August 2026 against the then-pinned Media3 1.5 bytecode; Koda keeps the resolution app-owned on Media3 1.11] Media3 maps one press to Play/Pause and two to Next, but treats a third press as the start of another single press, producing Next followed by a delayed Play/Pause. `HeadsetButtonSequence` preserves single and double press and adds triple press as Previous; double press necessarily settles after the same multi-press window so a third press can still win. Triple Previous always changes tracks rather than applying the ordinary three-second restart rule. Explicit Play/Pause, Next and Previous keys, notification controls, Android Auto and controller commands stay on Media3's existing paths.

**MusicService's session caches are bounded.** Resolved music URLs are an access-ordered 128-entry map and the successful warm bookkeeping keeps 256 ids; both may span hours of continuous listening, so an unbounded collection is a leak even though each entry is small. Evicting a warmed id is harmless because `CacheWriter` skips bytes already on disk. Live Update artwork ids are in-flight markers, removed when each load finishes, rather than a permanent record of every cover seen during the service lifetime.

**The player visualizer reads Koda's own PCM, and that is why it needs no permission.** [verified September 2026] The obvious implementation is `android.media.audiofx.Visualizer` pointed at the service's pinned audio session id - and it is the wrong one: that class is the platform's audio *capture* API, gated on RECORD_AUDIO whatever session it is aimed at, so it puts a microphone entry in the store listing and lights the privacy indicator while music plays. There is no exemption for your own output. But both engines already build their sink with an app-owned processor chain for the AutoMix filter (`MusicService.buildPlayer`), so the decoded samples are in hand on their way to the AudioTrack: `VisualizerAudioProcessor` sits after `TransitionFilterAudioProcessor` in that `setAudioProcessors` array, copies each buffer through untouched, and windows 1024 samples into `VisualizerMath`'s radix-2 FFT. It analyses only while something is drawing, so with the strip off the cost is one memcpy per PCM buffer. **A crossfade has two writers**, one per engine, and `VisualizerBus` merges them by taking the louder band - which is what an overlap sounds like, where last-writer-wins would flicker between two tracks for the length of every fade. The bus is process-wide and is deliberately *not* an eleventh entry on invariant 6's list: it holds no user data, persists nothing, is not backed up, and empties when nothing is drawing - it is transport, like the shared OkHttp dispatcher. Session extras were the first idea and are the wrong channel; they carry state that outlives the UI, not thirty arrays a second. The strip is **off by default** (it adds 88dp to eight layouts) and collapses to still bars under reduced motion or Battery Saver rather than vanishing, so the layout does not move underneath someone who turned it on.

**Lyrics layout is owned by `SyncedLyricsView`, not by each player style.** Every style gives it the full available pane; the shared view chooses one responsive gutter, line spacing and vertical runway. Do not put another lyric-only horizontal padding wrapper around it. [scar] The active line used to be enlarged with a post-layout `scale`, so its measured bounds stayed small while the drawing grew outside them; large app or system text scale then clipped words against the pane and adjacent lines. Emphasis now comes from measured typography and weight. Karaoke words measure against the actual pane, soft-wrap even one unbroken token, reserve side/top drawing insets for glyph bearings and motion, and preserve the displayed line as authoritative when provider timing omits final punctuation. Auto-scroll uses each wrapped row's measured centre rather than assuming a fixed-height line.

**Lyrics are set in Montserrat, and only where the song's own script allows it.** [judgement September 2026] The lyric is the one text in the app that is not in the user's language: a Korean release, a Hindi film song, an Arabic or Japanese track all arrive as the body of a screen whose chrome is English, and Montserrat publishes Latin, Latin Extended, Vietnamese and Cyrillic and nothing else. Asking for it unconditionally bets those songs' legibility on a platform fallback that is neither promised nor visible in review, and the failure mode is a screen of boxes on exactly the songs nobody testing in English opens. So `util/LyricsScript.kt` counts the *letters* of the fetched lyric - digits, punctuation and symbols say nothing about a script - and `SyncedLyricsView` asks for the display family only when at most 8% of them fall outside those blocks. Two details are deliberate: the decision is made **once per song**, because a mixed verse-and-chorus track would otherwise change typeface as it scrolled and the metric shift is worse than either font alone; and it is a **share rather than a single character**, because one stray CJK bracket or borrowed word would otherwise demote a whole English song, and for a handful of glyphs a platform fallback is a bonus rather than something relied on. `withLyricsFont` leaves the style untouched when the answer is no - `copy(fontFamily = null)` would mean the platform default rather than "inherit", which would quietly take every non-Latin song off the theme's font too. `LyricsScriptTest` holds the thresholds, with every non-Latin string written as escapes so a file saved in the wrong encoding cannot make the test pass for the wrong reason. The empty and loading states stay on the app's own font: they are chrome, not lyrics.

**Lyrics provider selection follows fresh settings.** [judgement September 2026] Local sidecars/tags still win before any network work. Player and download repositories read `LyricsConfiguration` for each lookup and include it in the cache key. Enabled providers run concurrently within one 9.5-second budget; completion order cannot defeat user priority. Synced preference ranks word, line, then plain text with provider order breaking ties; switching it off uses provider order alone. Plain-text exclusion applies to remote results. An early winner cancels outstanding requests only after higher-priority contenders finish. Timeout retains completed candidates.

**Last.fm scrobbling is optional and off by default.** `LastFmPlaybackTracker` samples the active player on the service main scope, follows occurrence ids across crossfade handoffs, and resets on repeat/privacy changes. It measures playing wall time rather than seek position. Queue, retries and account ownership live in `LastFmRepository`; details and unverified credential-backed paths are in `lastfm.md`.
