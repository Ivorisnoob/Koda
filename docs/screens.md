# Navigation and screens

Reference detail moved out of `CLAUDE.md` so it loads only when needed. `CLAUDE.md` keeps the summary, the invariants and the silent-failure index; this file keeps the reasoning and the scars. Markers (`[verified]`, `[scar]`, `[judgement]`, `[drifts]`) mean what `CLAUDE.md` says they mean. If a change contradicts a sentence here, fix the sentence in the same commit.

`MainActivity` hosts a `NavHost` (`onboarding` → `home`, plus `settings`, `downloads`, `stats`, `update`, and the channel route). The `home` route contains its own tab system - `AnimatedContent` plus an inline `HorizontalFloatingToolbar` built in `HomeScreen.kt`, not a separate nav-bar component - rather than nav routes. The expressive toolbar uses Material 3's own `exitAlwaysScrollBehavior(FloatingToolbarExitDirection.Bottom)` at the Home shell: every tab's nested scroll hides it downward and reverse scroll returns it, while the optional standard `NavigationBar` remains pinned. Do not replace that with list-index polling or a hand-rolled offset. **Whatever is parked above that toolbar follows it down**: the music pill and the video mini bar both read `FloatingToolbarState.hiddenFraction()` and translate by it, or scrolling a feed leaves a mini player hovering over the strip the toolbar vacated. Its `FloatingToolbarState` is hoisted to `MusicApp` for exactly that reason - the video overlay is a sibling of the NavHost and can read nothing `HomeScreen` provides. Read the fraction only inside a deferred lambda (`offset {}`, `graphicsLayer {}`); it changes every scroll frame, and reading it in composition recomposes the player - including its embedded video view - just as often. `listContentPadding` deliberately does **not** follow it: padding that changes because of scrolling feeds back into the scroll that changed it.

**A mode switch cross-fades; a tab move slides.** `AnimatedContent` is keyed on `HomeTabKey(tab, videoMode)` rather than the tab alone so the spec can tell them apart. Both Homes draw their header in the same place, so sliding sideways on a mode switch dragged that header off with the page and read as the whole chrome leaving, when nothing about it was meant to move. A global `videoMode` toggle swaps the Home tab between music content and `VideoHomeContent`, and tab 2 between Library and video history. Video-mode destinations retain stable tab ids while `VideoHomeConfiguration` controls their visible order; transition direction follows configured order, hidden-current state falls back to the first visible destination, and the preference layer refuses to hide the final one. **Hiding Home moves its top bar onto the shell** [scar]: the account avatar, downloads and Settings live in `VideoTopBarSection` inside `VideoHomeContent`, so hiding that one destination took away the only route to Settings in video mode - including the switch that puts Home back, which is itself inside Settings. `videoShellTopBar` draws the same bar above every destination in that configuration, and `VIDEO_SHELL_TOP_BAR_HEIGHT` is added to the one `listContentPadding` every tab scrolls inside so nothing hides underneath it. Anything that becomes reachable from only one destination needs the same treatment. Recommendations are a separate gate: disabling them clears and blocks the network Home feed without implicitly disabling Shorts or hiding Home. Both players are **overlays above the NavHost**, driven by their ViewModels' `isExpanded` state, which is why a NavHost destination like the channel screen needs them to step aside.

**An overlay above the NavHost cannot be drawn over by anything inside it, which is a layering rule and not only a z-order.** [scar] `VideoPlayerOverlay` is a sibling of the NavHost so a video survives navigation; the music player is inside `HomeScreen`. So the video's collapsed bar drew on top of the *full-screen* music player - watch a video, switch to music, play a song, open the player, and there is a mini bar for the video floating over it. Both bars surviving a mode switch is deliberate and stays; one of them hovering over the other's open player is not. `miniBarHidden` takes the bar off screen entirely rather than making it transparent, which also hands the video surface back so a bar nobody can see is not decoding, and the expanded video player is never suppressed because in that case it is the thing owning the window. It is driven by `PlayerViewModel.isPlayerExpanded`, which the music player's host mirrors into the ViewModel and clears on dispose as well as on collapse - the flag lives in Home's own `remember`, so leaving Home drops it and a stale true would hide the video bar for the rest of the session. Anything else that ends up owning the whole window needs the same wiring; the two layers share no scope and cannot discover each other any other way.

**A mode switch pauses the other player; it does not dismantle it.** [scar] `switchPlaybackMode` used to route the toggle through the players' own dismiss actions, and those are destructive on purpose - `clearPlayer` empties the queue and erases the last-played song *and* the saved session, `closePlayer` drops the video resume snapshot because an explicit close means "done with this video". A mode switch means neither: flicking over to look at videos is not finishing the album, and returning to a Home with no mini player and no route back to the queue is the app discarding work nobody asked it to discard. It now pauses music, collapses video through `setExpanded(false)` (the path that swaps an HDR rendition for its SDR twin, since the mini player is a TextureView) and pauses it. **Shorts is still closed outright**, having no minimised form to park in. Both mini players surviving at once is the state the layout was already built for and could not previously reach through this toggle: `videoMiniBottomChrome` stacks the video bar above the music pill and `bottomOverlayInset` reserves 196dp for the pair. It is safe because playback is already mutually exclusive by explicit rule rather than by audio focus - three `LaunchedEffect`s in `MusicApp` pause the other two whenever one starts, since focus alone is unreliable between players inside one app - and because both session restores arrive paused and collapsed.

**Search history follows the field across modes.** `SearchScreen` uses the same `SearchHistoryRepository` in music and video mode: focusing an empty field shows the recent-query surface, and clearing focus returns to the mode's ordinary empty-query content. Video's empty state is Trending only; do not re-add hard-coded Explore category chips, which duplicate ordinary queries and hide the user's own history.

**Classic Home needs three usable recommendations, not merely a non-empty parser result.** Its organic hero addresses indices 0, 1 and 2, so accepting one malformed or partial `FEmusic_home` row leaves the signature three-artwork layout visibly broken. `usableHomeRecommendations` removes blank/placeholder titles and duplicate ids; `getRecommendations` keeps valid primary rows, then fills from Liked Music and finally a generic search until the surface has enough. `HomeRecommendationCache` stores at most 30 metadata rows per profile for seven days, renders them immediately while a refresh runs, and is cleared on logout; an empty/error response never overwrites it. It is a cache, not backed-up user data. No InnerTube shape was changed for this repair: a signed-in live response could not be probed without a device session, so the existing parser was left alone rather than edited from inference.

**Spotlight's filter row is a segmented control, not chips** - five mutually exclusive views of one screen, which Material 3 Expressive draws as connected `ToggleButton`s with a shape morph on selection (the component SponsorBlock's category rows and the video quality tabs already use). Loose `FilterChip`s read as "pick any" and gave the selected one nothing but a fill. It scrolls rather than dividing the width five ways, because the labels are not the same length. **"For you" is the one filter that is not a view of the library**: `RecommendationEngine.getDiscoveryRecommendations` interleaves "latest songs" searches on the top artists (the half that surfaces recent releases), related-song radios seeded from favourites (the half that reaches sideways into artists the user has never played), and recent searches, then drops everything the caller already knows about - library, likes, play history - so what comes back is new rather than ranked-lower-but-familiar. There is deliberately **no browse id for new music** in it: only `FEmusic_home`, `FEmusic_liked_playlists` and `FEmusic_liked_videos` have been probed in this codebase, and inventing a fourth is writing a request from memory. It is fetched when its own tab is opened rather than with the rest of Home, since it costs several searches plus a radio call and most sessions never open it; an empty result never overwrites a shelf that is already showing something.

**Two music Homes, the second opt-in.** `spotlight_home` (off by default) swaps tab 0's music content from `YourMixContent` to `SpotlightHomeContent` - shortcut grid, paged quick picks, artwork shelves - chosen in onboarding's Look and feel page or Settings → Appearance → Home. It is a third branch in the same `when` the video toggle already uses, so the tab system, overlays and nav bar are untouched. A boolean rather than an enum on purpose: no third Home is planned. `isAutoMix` drops YouTube's machine-made mixes; nothing marks a playlist as generated, so the only signal is the title, and patterns are anchored on the whole name so a user's own "Late night mixtape" survives.

**Spin is a child page over the music Home, not a route** (`ui/home/SpinScreen.kt`). Tab 0's music content sits inside a `PredictiveBackStack` whose foreground is the wheel, so back peels it away to whichever Home is underneath and that Home keeps its scroll. `spinOpen` is declared inside that branch so leaving the tab closes it, as it closes a Library sub-screen, and it is reached where each Home already starts music: a die badge on Your Mix's Play button, sat the way the incognito badge sits on the profile avatar, and beside Play all in Spotlight's quick-picks header, which opens the wheel on the pool that header is showing (`SpotlightFilter.spinSource`). [judgement] A full-width entry card was tried first and read as a section of its own on both Homes. Both take a *required* spin callback - a nullable default is the per-call-site wiring trap. The wheel resolves from flows Home already holds; only the For you pool calls `loadDiscovery`, and only when chosen. [judgement, September 2026] The drum's rows are drawn from the list's own layout inside `graphicsLayer` (`drumFace`), so turning it redraws and never recomposes, and the list repeats its pool `SPIN_LAPS` times and opens in the middle lap so it turns freely both ways. A spin picks its landing slot before it moves and eases there on a tween, then springs back from a fifth-of-a-slot overshoot: a spring alone cannot be told how far to travel. Play queues the pool from the landed song, which is always in it. **The chosen-slot state rows read only changes when the wheel settles** (`-1` while `isScrollInProgress`) [scar]: the first version changed it on every crossing, so every visible row recomposed - and started and stopped its Play pill and record spin - for each slot a spin passed, which read as lag. The per-crossing haptic reads the raw centre in a `snapshotFlow` instead, outside composition. **Covers are prefetched at the drawn size** into the shared Coil loader with the exact request the row makes (`spinCoverModel`, fixed `SPIN_COVER_REQUEST_PX`, no crossfade), so a slot arriving mid-spin is a memory hit; `SongArtwork`'s two-layer high-res load was the other half of the lag.

**Opening a Library sub-screen from outside Library is a hand-off, not a navigation.** `LibraryContent` owns playlist and artist detail in its own state, so callers pass `initialArtist` / `initialPlaylist` and clear them through the matching `onInitial…Consumed` callback as the tab renders. Without the clear, returning to Library re-opens whatever was last handed over. The player and song menus use the artist and album halves; Spotlight, share links and stream albums the playlist half. **Backing out of a hand-off returns to the tab it began on** (`initialReturnToCaller` / `onReturnToCaller`, the tab recorded by `HomeScreen.noteLibraryReturn`), with the player left as it was. [scar] The first answer reopened the player sheet, which replayed the player's whole expand animation over a screen the user was leaving. The return holds for anything opened inside the excursion (an album from the artist), is not previewed because the peel would reveal a Library root it is not going to, and is off when the hand-off began in the Library or crossed a mode switch.

**A hand-off across the video toggle goes through `goToTab`, never an assignment to `selectedTab`.** [scar] Two separate things make this the only correct route, and each one on its own drops the user somewhere they did not ask for.

First, **a tab number does not name a screen**: tab 2 is the music Library while the toggle is off and the Subscriptions feed while it is on, and the video Library is 3. The player's `onArtistClick`/`onAlbumClick` set `selectedTab = 2` and nothing else, so the artist name in a music player opened over video mode landed on the Subscriptions feed - the music player outlives a mode switch, its mini bar stays, and expanding it from there is an ordinary thing to do.

Second - and this is what survived the obvious fix - **`selectedTab` is `rememberSaveable(videoMode)`**, deliberately, so that switching modes lands on that mode's own last tab (`homePreferences.getLastHomeTab`). The mode switch is a preference write and therefore lands a recomposition later, so the key *re-creates* the state after it and discards anything assigned before it: adding `onVideoModeToggle(false)` ahead of `selectedTab = 2` moved the failure from Subscriptions to the music Home, with the artist request still pending on the ViewModel. `goToTab(tab, wantVideoMode)` assigns directly when the mode already matches, and otherwise flips the mode and parks a `TabRequest(tab, videoMode)` that an effect applies once `videoMode` matches the request - so the request can never be spent on the mode it was trying to leave. The same mechanism carries the channel page's "open music artist page" cross-link, shared playlist links and the video-playlist hand-off, all of which had the same latent fault. `noteLibraryReturn()` still runs *before* `goToTab`, because it reads `videoMode` to decide whether back has a tab to return to.

## Device videos

The video Library's "On this device" entry (`ui/video/DeviceVideosPage.kt`, `data/LocalVideoRepository.kt`) reads MediaStore, groups by bucket into folder cards, and plays the files through the ordinary video player.

**Playback reuses the download path exactly.** `LocalPlaybackSource` generalises what `playDownloadedVideos` already had, so `_isLocalPlayback` still means "ExoPlayer is reading a file on this device" and everything gated on it - no `/next`, no YouTube playback report, no caption fetch, no HDR downgrade aimed at saving bandwidth that is not being spent - covers gallery files for free. A device video's id carries the `device:` prefix (`LocalVideo.playbackId`) because it travels through fields typed for YouTube ids; nothing may build a watch URL or an InnerTube request from one.

**Koda's own watch history is the one thing local playback does write.** `recordLocalWatch` applies the same rules the online path does - ten seconds of actual playback, a fresh `isSaveVideoHistoryEnabled` read - and stops there: nothing is reported to YouTube, because a device file is nobody's upload. Profile scope and incognito are enforced inside `VideoHistoryRepository.addVideo`, which is exactly why that gate lives in the store rather than at each call site. **The `device:` id is deliberately reversible** (`LocalVideo.uriFor`): history stores a `VideoItem` and nothing else, so replaying an entry and drawing its frame both have to get back to the file from the id alone - which is also why `playVideo` routes a device id to local playback itself instead of leaving each of the surfaces that open a video to remember. An externally opened one-off URI gets an `external:` id rather than a device one and is **not** recorded, since the read grant dies with the task and the row would be a dead link.

**One MainActivity, and picture-in-picture is why it has to be `singleTask`.** [scar] PiP moves the activity into a task of its own, which `singleTop` does not match, so an "open with" from a file manager - launched into a new task - started a *second* MainActivity: two `VideoPlayerViewModel`s and two ExoPlayers, the PiP window still playing while the new copy opened the file and lost the audio focus fight. That is the "it opens but does not play" report, and no amount of stopping playback inside one instance could have fixed it. `ExternalVideoHandler` is also deliberately not gated on PiP, unlike the shared-link handler beside it: a file hand-off is an explicit "play this now", and the window it replaces is the one still playing. [scar] The normal NavHost is removed from composition while PiP is active, so returning rebuilds whichever Home tab was underneath; Search's deliberate-on-entry autofocus then reopened the keyboard over the video player. Expanding video clears focus and hides the IME, and a Search screen rebuilt under an already-expanded player suppresses that one initial focus request.

**Koda appears in "Open with" for video files, and hands files back out the same way.** The manifest claims `ACTION_VIEW` on `video/*` over the `content` and `file` schemes plus `ACTION_SEND` for a share; both are re-checked at handling time (`Intent.externalVideoUri`), because the filters match by mime type and a chooser can pass something else. `MainActivity.takeSharedLink` checks for a file *before* the link path - an "open with" puts the video in the same `data` field a YouTube link uses, and the link path would have called it "no YouTube link found". A URI that resolves back to a MediaStore row (`LocalVideo.playbackIdFor`) is treated as an ordinary device video, id and all. **Koda offers no delete**, and `openVideoWithExternalApp` is why it does not need one: the system chooser over `ACTION_VIEW` reaches the gallery or file manager, which already has the system confirmation and the user's own trash behind it.

**Quality and HDR are read back from the decoder, not from MediaStore.** The provider records dimensions inconsistently and says nothing about dynamic range, so the row starts as a placeholder and `applyLocalVideoFormat` renames it from the selected video track on the first `onTracksChanged`. HDR10 and HLG come from the track's transfer function and Dolby Vision from its codec - `VideoDynamicRange.DOLBY_VISION` exists only for this path, since YouTube never serves it but phone cameras record it constantly.

**Embedded audio and subtitle tracks are selectable** (`data/MediaTrackLabels.kt`, `publishSelectableTracks`). Overrides resolve against the player's live `Tracks` rather than a remembered group, because a track list belongs to the media item that produced it. Three rules worth keeping: a track the device cannot decode is left out rather than offered and then producing silence; the audio section appears only above one track, since one is not a choice; and subtitles open *off* unless captions were left on, enforced by disabling the track type - clearing the override alone lets the selector re-pick a container's default-flagged track, so subtitles the user just switched off come straight back.

**Thumbnails go through a small Coil fetcher, deliberately** (`data/LocalVideoThumbnail.kt`). `coil-video`'s decoder works from an `ImageSource`, meaning Coil fetches the file first - copying a multi-gigabyte film into the cache to draw a 200dp card. `ContentResolver.loadThumbnail` serves MediaStore's own pre-generated frame instead, with one scaled keyframe decode as the fallback for files the system scanner could not open. It needs a `Keyer` as well as a `Fetcher`: a request whose key is null skips the memory cache entirely.

**Access is asked for from the Library, not through a setting**, which is what keeps a permission dialog away from anyone who never opens the section - the same reasoning as the `load_local_songs` scar. Android 14's partial grant is a first-class state (`LocalVideoAccess.PARTIAL`): `READ_MEDIA_VISUAL_USER_SELECTED` is declared so the picker offers "Select videos" at all, the chosen files are shown with a way to widen the selection, and a wall over content the user has already shared would be the wrong answer. **Local Only mode hosts `DeviceVideosScreen` directly** in place of the notice that used to stand there, which is why the folder step lives in that composable rather than in the Library's page enum.

## Three kinds of playlist

Not interchangeable, in either mode. **Local** (`PlaylistRepository`, songs embedded, fully editable), **the account's own** (InnerTube, editable through it), and **saved** (`data/SavedPlaylistsRepository.kt`) - somebody else's, kept as a reference (id, url, title, author, artwork, count) and re-fetched live on every open, never copied. `HomeViewModel.userPlaylists` merges all three and `savedPlaylistIds` tells the third apart, deliberately excluding anything the account already owns. **That distinction is load-bearing** [scar]: a saved playlist's id starts with `PL` like any other, so without it the UI offers Rename, Delete and track editing on a playlist the user has no rights to, and those writes fail at the endpoint after the UI has already moved. Saved playlists never enter the add-to-playlist sheet, because you cannot add tracks to someone else's playlist. **A `PL` prefix says an id is a real YouTube playlist, not that it is yours** - both detail pages ask whether the library actually holds the playlist rather than reading the prefix. **Anything that is not already local can be copied into one** (`HomeViewModel.copyPlaylistToLocal`, offered from the playlist page's overflow once its tracks are on screen) - an album and a generated mix included, since a snapshot of either is a fine starting point. The copy drops duplicate ids rather than carrying them: a YouTube playlist may legitimately list the same video twice, but `PlaylistRepository.removeSongFromPlaylist` filters by id, so one copy removed would take all of them - a bug met while doing the very editing the copy exists for. Its cover is generated from the name like any other local playlist rather than lifted from the original, which belongs to whoever published it.

**The playlist page is cover-led, and the title is not on the cover.** `PlaylistDetailScreen` - one screen behind all three kinds, plus albums and Ready offline - opens on the artwork edge to edge under the status bar, near-square but capped at 44% of the window so the controls and the first track still land above the fold in landscape, and asked for at `HERO_COVER_PX` because a playlist thumbnail arrives from a feed at around 120px and stretches to a smear. **Its foot fades into the ground with a plain gradient in the ground's own colour**, which is only correct because that ground is one flat value - there is exactly one colour to match, so a SrcOver fade lands on it precisely. This was first built as a `DstIn` mask that erased the cover's foot instead, on the reasoning that erasing cannot mis-match whatever is behind it. **On screen it composited to black** [scar] and ended the cover on a hard dark edge: the layer was not isolating the way `CompositingStrategy.Offscreen` promises, and `DstIn` against an opaque backdrop blackens where it should clear. A blend mode that fails this way fails silently and only on a device, so the flat-ground fade is the one to keep. The title, the facts, Play/Shuffle, the save/download row and the description then stack on that ground on one 24dp rail.

**The ground is one flat colour, not a gradient** (`playlistPageGround`). [judgement] A wash that decays down the page ends with the bottom of a long track list back on the plain theme, which reads as the colour having run out rather than as a gradient, and it gives the cover's fade a moving target so the hand-off can only line up at one depth. One colour that the cover blends into and the page then holds has neither problem. Everything that must agree with it resolves it through that one function - the page behind the list, the app bar fading in over the rows, the status-bar scrim on the cover - so they match by construction rather than by three constants kept in step by hand.

**That colour is mixed from `primaryContainer` toward `primary`, and never with the background.** [scar] It was twice built as a blend *between the background and `primaryContainer`*, at 0.45 and then at 0.8, and both read as plain white on screen. `primaryContainer` is a pale tone by construction - tone 90 in a light theme - so anything blended between it and a tone-98 background sits within a few percent of the background at **any** blend value; there is no strength that formula could have been raised to. Going toward `primary` instead leaves the pale end in both themes at once: `primary` is a deep tone 40 in a light scheme, so the ground darkens into real colour, and a bright tone 80 in a dark one, so it lifts out of the near-black.

**On a mid-tone ground the M3 elevation rule inverts** (`playlistRaisedSurface`). A container is normally a step toward the middle from an extreme background, but there is nowhere to step to from the middle, and a card that *darkens* on a coloured page reads as a hole punched in it rather than as something lying on top. Raised surfaces here therefore always lift toward the light, which needs the theme, because the light end of the cover's palette is a different role in each - `primaryContainer` in a light scheme, `primary` in a dark one. It is probed off the background's luminance, the same test `rememberArtworkColorScheme` uses to pick its tones. The title and facts move to `onPrimaryContainer` for the same reason: `onBackground` is the contrast pair for a theme background that is no longer behind them. The controls stay on the accent *roles* (`primary` for Play, `secondaryContainer` for the tonal ones), which land on opposite sides of a mid-tone ground instead of vanishing into a pastel one - the saturated ground is what buys the buttons their contrast, not what threatens it. With artwork colors off the whole arrangement resolves to the app's own palette and still holds together. **The title sits below the cover, not on it**, which is where this page parts company with `ArtistHero`: a name can be set over an artist photo because a portrait has a predictable shape, while a playlist cover is any image at all including a white one, and no scrim makes type over it reliable across every cover. Accents come from `rememberArtworkColorScheme` - the player's own extractor - gated on the same **Album Art Colors** setting, because a page that tints itself from the cover while that switch is off is the switch not working. The scaffold inset is dropped deliberately so the cover draws under the transparent bar; M3 `Scaffold` places body content *before* `topBar`, so back/search/overflow still draw over it, on a top fade that gives them ground.

**Creating a local playlist is a screen, not a dialog** (`ui/library/PlaylistStudioScreen.kt`, `LibraryRoute.CreatePlaylist`, opened from the Library FAB menu). Name over a live generated-cover preview on top, seed chips underneath, songs below, a docked create bar with the selection tray. Three judgements worth keeping: **the cover preview and the generator share one color system** (`data/PlaylistCoverArt.kt`) - same single-hue tonal scheme, same four motif families, so what the preview promises is what the bitmap delivers, though never pixel-identically: the real cover is seeded by the playlist's random UUID, which does not exist until create, so the preview seeds from the *name's* hash. The single-hue rule is deliberate [judgement]: the first generator blended primary into tertiary corner-to-corner at pinned saturation, which on most palettes is the stock "AI gradient" purple melt; a cover is now a tonal ramp of one seed hue with the other seed as a single accent disc, and near-neutral (Graphite-style) seeds keep their own saturation rather than having a meaningless hue resolved into red. A custom cover can also be chosen right in the studio - camera badge on the preview, applied through the awaited `applyLocalPlaylistCover` *before* navigation so the new page never flashes the generated art; **"genres you listen to" is an approximation by construction** [judgement] - nothing this app reads from YouTube carries genre metadata, so the chips are the taste profile's top artists, favorites and recent searches (`HomeViewModel.buildPlaylistSeedProfile`) plus a curated genre list backed by ordinary music searches, no new InnerTube shapes; and **every picked song earns one related-songs shelf, once** - the existing `/next` radio call keyed by the picked id, capped per pick and in total, so a big selection cannot fan out into unbounded fetches. A song already visible is filtered out of later shelves at render, which also keeps lazy keys unique. Creating with zero songs is valid - `createLocalPlaylistWithSongs` is not `copyPlaylistToLocal`.

**"Add from your playlists" is a Library sub-route below the playlist, not a sheet** (`ui/library/PlaylistImportScreen.kt`, `LibraryRoute.ImportSongs`). A multi-select list with sort and select-all is exactly the content the bottom-sheet silent ceiling exists for, so it is a screen. Any source in the library is offered - the account's playlists, saved references, Liked Songs - because the songs are *copied into* the user's own local playlist, the read `copyPlaylistToLocal` already performs. Songs already in the target render disabled rather than being hidden (a silently shorter list reads as a broken fetch), and the write is one batch (`PlaylistRepository.addSongsToPlaylist`) that deduplicates by id again, because each save rewrites the whole playlist file. Navigation-wise it is the Library's one two-level route: `back()` sends it to `LibraryRoute.Playlist` before the excursion check runs, it is excluded from predictive-back previewing (the peel would reveal the Library root while back actually lands on the playlist), and the source-picked step's in-place back is a plain inner `BackHandler`. The playlist detail wiring is `onAddSongsRequest`, deliberately without a default - the per-call-site null-default trap - offered in the overflow and as the local playlist's empty-state action.

**Hiding a playlist is local, and it is not unsubscribing.** `data/HiddenPlaylistsRepository.kt` (prefs `hidden_playlists`, device-wide like every other playlist store) holds ids the user told Koda not to list, with enough of each name to be lifted by name rather than by opaque id. The two cases it exists for are a playlist of videos that is meaningless in music mode and a playlist on the account the user simply does not want in front of them - neither is a request to change anything on YouTube, so nothing here writes to InnerTube and the playlist stays reachable by link, by search, and by anything already playing it. It is applied to the *merged* list in `HomeViewModel.userPlaylists` rather than to each source, so one id covers the playlist wherever it came from, and it is **a filter over the fetch, never a write into it** - the `NotInterestedRepository` rule, for the same reason: un-hiding has to bring the playlist straight back rather than depend on a refetch. `videoPlaylists` filters on the same set, because a playlist id means the same playlist whichever list found it and a control labelled "Hide from Koda" that stops at one tab is the UI lying. **The un-hide sheet is not optional**: the count beside the Library's playlist header is the only route back, which is why that header now stands even when every playlist is hidden - without it a mis-tap weeks ago is an invisible hole in the library, exactly what `NotInterestedScreen` exists to prevent. The hide row is offered on **every** playlist card including the ones with nothing else in their menu, since the synthesized Supermix and Your Likes entries cannot be renamed, deleted or unsaved and are precisely the rows people want gone.

**Video mode got its local kind last** (`data/LocalVideoPlaylistsRepository.kt`, videos embedded, one JSON file per playlist under `filesDir/video_playlists`). [scar] Until it existed, `videoPlaylists` was the account's list alone, so signed out the save sheet listed nothing, every long-press routed to a sign-in wall, and the pinned Watch Later row posted to an endpoint that answers 200 without a session and does nothing - the sheet reported a save that had not happened. Both `HomeViewModel.videoPlaylists` and `VideoPlayerViewModel.videoPlaylists` are now `combine(local, account)` with local first, and **the routing decision lives in `addVideoToPlaylist` rather than at the six surfaces that open the sheet**: a local id, or `"WL"` while signed out (resolving to a device Watch Later created on first use), or the account. The `localvp_` prefix exists precisely because that one id string reaches `addToYouTubePlaylist`, the playlist browse, the share-URL builder and the saved-playlist filter, each of which must branch. **Nothing may assume a `VideoPlaylist.playlistId` addresses YouTube.** A local playlist is a copy with no upstream, which is what separates it from a saved one, so `loadPlaylistVideos` serves it straight from the store.

**The saved store is one library across both modes.** Video mode saves into the same list and reads it back through `SavedPlaylist.toVideoPlaylist()`, so a playlist kept from video search is in the music Library grid too. A YouTube playlist id is the same id whichever page found it, and a per-mode store would make "saved" mean two things and let one id be saved twice. `getPlaylist(id)` resolves any playlist id through `https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=`, which is why `SavedPlaylist.url` is stored in that canonical form. Albums cross over: a video-mode open of an MPRE id resolves through `loadPlaylistVideos`'s album branch rather than the playlist call a browse id cannot answer, and their share link is the `/browse/` form. Saved rows **render signed out**, which is why the login card sits underneath the list rather than replacing it.

**Playlist download inventory has two different counts and must name both.** A playlist is an ordered list of entries and may contain the same video id several times; offline storage is keyed by that id and correctly downloads one reusable file. Already-downloaded ids likewise reduce new transfer work without reducing the playlist. `MusicPlaylistDownloadAction` therefore labels its header action with the full playlist row count, while `PlaylistDownloadSheet` separately reports already-offline, repeated-entry and remaining unique-file counts. Never surface `distinctBy(id).size` as if it were the playlist's song count.

**The manual filesystem scan walks one tree, and proving that needs canonical paths.** [scar] The OEM/HyperOS workaround (`load_local_songs` manual scan, off by default) took its roots from `EXTERNAL_STORAGE`, `/storage/emulated/0` and `/sdcard` and deduplicated them with `distinct()` on the raw strings. On nearly every device `EXTERNAL_STORAGE` *is* `/sdcard`, which symlinks onto `/storage/emulated/0`, so `distinct()` removed only the literal repeat and left two names for one tree - and the pass meant to catch that keyed on `absolutePath.hashCode()`, which differs between the two visits. Every song on the device was listed twice, with every artist and album count doubled to match; the report reads as "duplicates in the artist categorisation" and has nothing to do with multi-artist tags, which group by the whole artist string and cannot duplicate a row. `resolveScanRoots` canonicalises before deduplicating, `scanDir` carries a visited set of canonical directories (a symlink pointing back up its own tree is the same bug wearing a hat, and recurses until the stack goes), and the result is keyed by canonical path rather than by that 32-bit hash, which could silently drop a song on a collision. **Canonicalising is also what makes folder exclusion work on that path at all**: excluded folders come from `getAvailableFolders`, which reads MediaStore's `DATA` column and so stores `/storage/emulated/0/...`, which a scan walking `/sdcard/...` could never match - and `isExcludedFolder` is now one rule shared with the MediaStore branch, so excluding a folder excludes its subfolders in both rather than only in one. `SongScanTest` covers the alias collapse, a real second volume surviving it, and the exclusion prefix rules.

**Device albums follow tag order, not the library query order.** `SongRepository` reads TRACK, CD_TRACK_NUMBER and DISC_NUMBER and stores normalized positive `trackNumber` / `discNumber` on `Song`; Android's legacy TRACK value encodes disc 2 track 7 as 2007, while modern tags may be strings such as `7/12`. `sortedInAlbumOrder()` puts tagged songs in disc-then-track order, resolves duplicate positions by title/id, and leaves untagged files last in deterministic title order. Apply it only when opening or playing an album: the all-songs library still owns its user-selected sort. The manual filesystem scan reads the same tags with `MediaMetadataRetriever` because bypassing MediaStore must not mean discarding the tracklist.

## The video options sheet

**`ui/video/VideoOptionsSheet.kt` is two panes, not one scroll.** Pane 1 is a fixed action list (Play next, Add to queue, Watch later, Save to playlist, Download, Go to channel, Share, and the two muted "stop recommending" rows); pane 2 is the playlist picker, whose entire body is one `LazyColumn` with the search field above and New playlist / Done pinned below. [scar] That split is the whole point of the file: the previous single-column version laid roughly 1100dp of fixed rows into a sheet that gets about 890dp, in a `Column` with no scroll, so the bottom rows were clipped with nothing logged - and because the inner playlist list was capped at 340dp, the overflow was identical at zero playlists and at three hundred. **A bottom sheet whose content does not scroll has a silent hard ceiling, and an inner scroller does not save an outer non-scroller.** Do not fold the picker back into the actions pane, and do not add a section to pane 1 without checking the height; pane 1 scrolls only as a landscape backstop, not as room to grow.

Saving is add-only and multi-target - the picker stays open so a video can go into several playlists, and only the terminal rows (Watch later, the two queue actions) close the sheet behind their check. `alreadyIn` covers **device playlists only**, deliberately: the account's would cost a browse call per row for a checkmark, and saving twice is a no-op on both sides.

**Every surface goes through `VideoOptionsSheetHost`** [scar], which wires the sheet and the download hand-off to a `HomeViewModel`. The player keeps its own call, since it drives `VideoPlayerViewModel` and conditions two actions on the playing video. The host exists because per-call-site wiring is what let the Home feed ship with `onEnqueueVideo` unset - the parameter defaults to null, a null hides the whole queue section, and nothing compile-fails. A new surface is three lines and cannot be half-wired.
