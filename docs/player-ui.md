# Player styles, queues and option sheets (`ui/player/`)

Reference detail moved out of `CLAUDE.md` so it loads only when needed. `CLAUDE.md` keeps the summary, the invariants and the silent-failure index; this file keeps the reasoning and the scars. Markers (`[verified]`, `[scar]`, `[judgement]`, `[drifts]`) mean what `CLAUDE.md` says they mean. If a change contradicts a sentence here, fix the sentence in the same commit.

**Motion artwork is a hero-art opt-in, not a change to every `SongArtwork`.** `MotionArtwork.kt` owns the expanded player's single muted, audio-disabled decoder and `PlayerArtwork` layers it over the existing static fallback. Only the incoming style and current song receive it; Gesture's neighbouring cards, queues, the mini-player and backgrounds remain static. `MotionArtworkRepository` is player-host scoped (not process-global); its bounded in-memory match cache stores no listening history on disk, and only its expiring public Apple web token and a 32 MB media cache persist under `cacheDir`. `MotionArtworkResolver` matches catalog songs to their actual album relationships and pins one concrete rendition rather than handing an adaptive master to the ABR selector. **How large a rendition is a four-tier picker** (`MotionArtworkQuality`, persisted by `name` and therefore frozen): Saver ~360px, Balanced ~480px at its cheapest encode - the long-standing behaviour and still the default, so no existing install moves - High the largest H.264 rung, Max whatever the ladder carries. [verified September 2026, 346 rungs across 12 square ladders] every rung Apple publishes is `VIDEO-RANGE=SDR`, H.264 stops at 1080x1080 and HEVC continues to 2160x2160, so **there is no HDR tier to offer** and Max is a codec step rather than a dynamic-range one - which is just as well, since the artwork surface is a TextureView and could not present HDR. The resolver returns an ordered **fallback chain** rather than one URL, because Max hands the device a 2160 HEVC clip not every decoder will take and a single playback error used to drop that song to its static cover for good; the chain only ever steps downward, so a failure costs sharpness and never extra data. The lookup cache and the media cache are both keyed or sized by tier - a cache keyed on the track alone would go on serving the old rendition for six hours after the picker moved. The setting defaults off, unmetered Wi-Fi only defaults on, and lifecycle, Battery Saver, Data Saver, animation-scale zero, Local Only and Incognito gate both lookup and playback. Pausing the music freezes the loop on its last frame rather than dropping to the still cover; music playback state only gates whether the loop advances. Keep this separate from YouTube video playback and audio focus. Apple lookup failures are static artwork, never a playback failure; don't add an opaque proxy or hardcoded bearer as a fallback. [scar] The token scrape shipped on a client built `followRedirects(false)`, and `https://music.apple.com` answers its own root with a **301** to a locale path - so `webToken()` threw on every call, `resolve()` swallowed it, and the entire feature was a silent no-op on every device and every song while looking correct in review. Two things fixed it and both matter: `getWeb` follows redirects itself, checking each hop against `MotionArtworkResolver.isWebUrl` so the client can go on refusing to follow them blindly, and the feature now logs its outcome under the `MotionArtwork` tag. It had **no logging at all**, which is why a dead feature took a live HTTP probe rather than one logcat line to diagnose. Probe the provider before trusting any of this shape - the same rule `youtube-data.md` states for InnerTube applies to Apple.

**The Canvas field is sampled from the cover's edge, and that is the whole trick.** The art fills the top `CANVAS_ART_FRACTION` of the screen and dissolves at its foot into `ui/player/CanvasField.kt`, whose top colour is the average of the artwork's own bottom rows in three columns - so the join is continuous by construction rather than disguised. Two cheaper answers were built first and both failed on the same point: a blurred copy of the cover still carries the cover's edges and composition, so the eye finds where the sharp and blurred versions line up however soft the mask, and a palette-derived cloud supplies a plausible colour rather than *the* colour, which reads as a step at the boundary. [scar] Reaching for `ChromaticMistBackground` also exposed a live defect worth knowing before reusing it anywhere: it early-returns a **flat `fallbackColor`** when `albumArtUrl` is null, and all four of its callers pass `highResThumbnailUrl`, which is null for every device-library song - so on a local file it paints a plain rectangle rather than an ambient background. `CanvasField` reads `thumbnailUrl ?: albumArtUri`, as `SongArtwork` does. Canvas also does not auto-hide its chrome: a full-bleed style is where controls are most wanted and were least often present.

**Naming trap: the `POSTER` enum key is the "Canvas" player.** The old kinetic-type Poster style was rewritten in place as full-bleed album art; the constant and file name (`PosterPlayerContent.kt`) kept the old key for prefs compatibility while every user-facing string says "Canvas". Searching "Canvas" will not find the enum; searching "Poster" lands on a file that does nothing poster-like. **Do not "fix" this** - the stored value is the string `"POSTER"` and renaming resets every user's style.

**Hero (`HERO`, `HeroPlayerContent.kt`) takes its colours from the player scheme and picks none.** [judgement, September 2026] The expanded player's `MaterialTheme` is already derived from the cover, so the deck is `primaryContainer`, the disc `primary` and the ink `onPrimaryContainer`. The art fades into the deck at its foot and the title sits on that fade in the deck's ink, rather than white type on a black scrim as Canvas does, so it reads on any cover in both themes. The play disc's shape is the play state (squared shoulders while playing, a circle while paused); its corner is clamped at 50% because the bouncy spring overshoots past a circle.

Adding or renaming a style touches **four** places (an omission compiles but misbehaves): the `PlayerStyle` constant in `ThemePreferences.kt`; a `<Name>PlayerSheetContent(viewModel, ...)` composable in `ui/player/<Name>PlayerContent.kt`; a branch in `ExpandablePlayer.kt`'s `when (playerStyle)`, the only dispatch point; and `playerStyleCatalog` in `PlayerStyleCatalog.kt` (label, subtitle, icon, and the `MaterialShapes` polygon that is the style's die-cut identity in the wheel). **The catalog is the only list** - settings picker, onboarding and `rememberPlayerStyleWheelEntries()` all derive from it. A new style also owes its action row an overflow button in that style's own primitive, last in the row, opening `NowPlayingOptionsSheet`; nothing compile-fails without one, it just quietly has no route to radio, artist, album or share. That used to be three hardcoded lists and the way one went stale. A new style also wants a branch in `PlayerStylePicker`'s `PlayerStylePreview`; that `when` is exhaustive, so a missing one is a compile error rather than a silent gap.

**Swipe-to-skip is one contract in `SwipeToSkip.kt`, not a per-style gesture.** `rememberSwipeToSkip(onNext, onPrevious)` plus `Modifier.swipeToSkip(state)` and `swipeToSkipFollow(state, factor)`; one state is shared by a style's artwork and its title/artist block, so both commit at the same 90dp threshold with the same spring home, and a drag crossing between them cannot skip twice (Compose keeps the gesture with the node that took the down). [scar] It exists because the gesture had been copied by hand into each style and **three of the eight - Classic, Bento, Dial - silently never got one**, while Morph drifted to its own threshold. Two styles keep their own reaction on purpose: **Sticker's peel** throws the art off and slaps the next on (its identity, not a spring-back), and **Gesture's swipe steps the queue** through `onQueueItemChange` because its carousel follows `currentIndex` and would fight a ViewModel skip. **The mini player does not use `SwipeToSkip`** - its skip is a carousel inside a pill that stays put (`MiniSkipCarousel`), because the pill is also the thing the vertical drags move, and dismiss moved to swipe down.

**The seek bar is one visual in `ExpressiveScrubber.kt`, and the styles kept their gesture.** Six styles drew `LinearWavyProgressIndicator` with a transparent `Slider` laid over it for touch, so `ExpressiveScrubber` is a drop-in replacement for the *indicator* only: the Slider still owns the drag, the value range, the seek and the accessibility semantics, and publishes the gesture through `LocalPlayerScrubInteraction` for the visual underneath to read. [judgement] Issue #250 asked for a component that replaces the whole stack; taking the gesture off the primary playback control in six styles that each express position differently - two in fractions, four in milliseconds, three calling `viewModel.seekTo` and three an `onSeekTo` lambda - is a much larger risk than the delight is worth, and none of the delight needs it. On touch the thumb blooms from a resting pill into a taller playhead on a bouncy spring, the wave settles flat because a wave travelling under a moving finger reads as the bar lagging behind it, and the waveform bars swell under a soft bell centred on the playhead. **The scrub ticks, and it goes through the app's own haptics.** [scar] This one control called `LocalHapticFeedback` directly, so it went on buzzing for somebody who had set Haptics to off - the exact failure `KodaHaptics` exists to make impossible - and its release effect fired on first composition, giving a confirm buzz every time the player opened or a style crossfaded in, for a bar nobody had touched. Both go through `rememberKodaHaptics` now, and holding the bar ticks once per detent as the playhead crosses it: the detents are the drawn bars taken every third one (10.5dp, about thirty across a phone-width track), because one per bar is a hundred pulses the vibrator smears into a single tone, with a 20ms floor so a flick cannot queue pulses that arrive after the gesture. It reads the *drawn* position rather than the raw gesture, so the tick and the bar passing under the playhead are one event, the rule the video player's level slats already follow; each style feeds a spring-smoothed fraction, so the offset from the finger is constant while a drag moves and what the hand feels is the cadence. The subscription is scoped to the gesture in its own composable, since `progress` changes every frame of playback and a collector left running would wake on all of them. Still missing from #250: chapter and SponsorBlock detents (music mode has neither) and the die-cut `MaterialShapes` bloom, which lost to a pill because the styles give this row only 12-14dp. **Dial and Bento are deliberately not converted** - Dial's progress is a circular dial and Bento's is a tile - and Poster's Slider is visible rather than transparent, so it would need its own treatment.

**A scrub leaves a return point, and coming back to it sticks.** [judgement September 2026] `ScrubReturnPoint` (`ui/player/ScrubReturnPoint.kt`) records where a drag started; the bar draws a two-tone dot there once the finger has left, and inside 10dp of it the value *is* the origin until the finger passes 18dp (hysteresis, so an edge cannot chatter). It arms only after the finger has left past the exit distance, because a magnet live from the first frame would make a small seek impossible from the thumb. **Releasing on the marker cancels the seek** rather than seeking to the origin: the song kept playing during the drag, so the origin is seconds stale, and "never mind" should not rewind or rebuffer. The music styles reach it through `LocalScrubReturnPoint` (provided by `ExpandablePlayer` beside the scrub interaction): each of the six `ExpressiveScrubber` styles routes its Slider's `onValueChange` through `follow` and asks `release()` before seeking, and the scrubber supplies the geometry (it alone knows its width) and draws the marker. The video `PlayerSeekBar` holds its own instance. Motion, haptics (`threshold` on landing, `subtle` on leaving, through `KodaHaptics`) and drawing are shared in `rememberScrubReturnMarker` / `drawScrubReturnMarker`, so the two bars cannot drift apart. The pure rule is `ScrubReturn.step`, covered by `ScrubReturnTest`. Dial, Bento and Poster draw their own bars and do not have it; `PlayerScreen.kt` is not composed anywhere.

**The position the bar draws is interpolated, not sampled.** [judgement September 2026] `PlayerViewModel` publishes `progress` once a second, which is the right rate for a value the player host, the mini bar and all nine styles collect in composition and the wrong rate for the one control whose whole job is to show motion - and every style had reached for the same wrong fix, an `animateFloatAsState` spring chasing each sample, which eases and lags and then jumps, so the thumb visibly stepped once a second. `rememberSmoothProgress` (`ui/player/PlayerProgress.kt`) extrapolates from the last sample at the rate the track is playing and re-anchors on the next one: playback is a clock, so a position plus a speed is the position at the next frame, and the bar is exact to the millisecond once a second while travelling at a constant truthful rate in between. Raising the sample rate was the alternative and is worse twice over - sixty samples a second is sixty recompositions a second of the host, the mini player and every style, which is precisely the scar `rememberPlayerWaveform` exists to undo, and it buys nothing the clock already knows. **Read the returned state only inside a deferred lambda** (`progress = { smooth.value }`, a draw scope, a graphics layer); a site that reads `.value` in composition has quietly moved its whole style onto the frame clock. Dial's read is inside its `Canvas` draw scope for that reason, and Bento's fill and Canvas's visible Material thumb are the two that do recompose per frame - both were already doing so under their springs, so neither is a regression. The rate arrives as `LocalPlaybackSpeed`, provided once by `ExpandablePlayer` beside the waveform and the scrub interaction rather than threaded through nine styles and the private composables inside them. A drag still wins outright: while `scrubPositionMs` is non-null the fraction *is* the finger, written from a `SideEffect` so a gesture does not relaunch a coroutine per frame. Extrapolation is capped at 1100ms, a little over one sample interval, so a stall or a rebuffer parks the thumb slightly ahead instead of sailing it across a bar that is not moving. **The mini player's progress ring is deliberately left on the sample**: it is a 52dp ring, one second of a three-minute song is about a pixel of arc, and it is on screen whenever anything plays - spinning the frame clock on the Home screen for an invisible gain is the wrong trade.

**A waveform is the whole song, decoded once at load, and then it holds still.** [scar] It was measured only from Koda's own playing PCM, which meant the bar could describe nothing past the playhead: a first listen watched the shape grow and re-scale behind the cursor for three minutes, and the one thing a waveform is for - seeing that the chorus is loud *before* reaching it - was the one thing it could not do. `WaveformAnalyzer` decodes the track in one `MediaExtractor`/`MediaCodec` pass and publishes a complete envelope through `WaveformEnvelope.measured`, which interpolates any bucket the decoder skipped so the result is genuinely `isComplete` rather than nearly so. **A stream is read through the ordinary playback source, and that is the whole design**: a `MediaDataSource` over `CacheManager`'s cache-backed Media3 `DataSource`, opened under the song's own cache key, so invariant 3's bounded ranged requests and `uaForPlaybackUri`'s per-client User-Agent both hold without the analyzer knowing about either, and the bytes it fetches are the same bytes playback is about to read - a song listened through costs nothing extra and starts from disk. Handing the extractor an https URL instead would be an unbounded GET with the wrong UA for bytes nothing else could use. [verified September 2026 against the Media3 1.11.0 bytecode] Reading the song the player is reading is safe rather than merely unlikely to collide: the factory sets no `FLAG_BLOCK_ON_CACHE`, so `CacheDataSource` takes `startReadWriteNonBlocking` and falls through to upstream without caching when the other reader holds the span - no lock waited on, no span written twice. Nothing may set that flag. [judgement October 2026] **The waveform switch is the consent, and only Local Only refuses a stream.** The pass used to be refused while the music cache was off, because the bytes could not be kept and it became a second download; the result was a setting that was on and a bar that stayed plain with nothing saying why. With the cache off it now reads through the cache without writing to it (`createCacheDataSourceFactory(writeEnabled = false)`, the rule playback follows for that switch) and costs that second download. A song already whole in the cache is measured even in Local Only, which forbids the network and not the disk. **There is deliberately no metered check**, and an earlier version of this had one: a connection-shaped rule hid the feature outright from anyone mostly on mobile data. [scar October 2026] **A pass that failed was never tried again, and one cut short was kept.** `request` latched the song before the work ran, so one dropped connection left the plain bar for as long as the song stayed current; `retryLater` now releases the latch after 1.5, 4 and 10 seconds and then gives up, since an unreadable container fails the same way every time. And a read that breaks reaches `MediaExtractor` as an ordinary end of stream, so the decode finished cleanly on half a song, `measured()` filled the tail flat, and the envelope was stored as complete - never to be measured again. The `MediaDataSource` records a failed read and that result is thrown away. The same source keeps the first 64 KB in memory and reads through a forward jump under 256 KB, because choosing a container is several extractors each reading the head from the top and every one of those had been a new ranged request. **Once the playing song is measured the next one is** (`analyzeSongsWhileEnabled`, music cache on only, where those bytes are what the next song then plays from); a request for the playing song replaces any other, so looking ahead cannot delay the bar on screen. A refusal is not a failure - the song keeps the plain wavy bar and gains its shape from being listened to, because `WaveformTap`'s live sampling stays as the fallback for exactly this and for containers no decoder here can open. **`WaveformStore.record` is a no-op once an envelope is complete**, since the two paths measure different signals - the file's own PCM against what the sink was handed after gain and normalisation - and letting a listening sample raise a bucket of an analysed envelope pulls one song's shape out of shape across replays. **The drawn envelope is a frozen snapshot, pinned per song.** `rememberPlayerWaveform` returns null until the store holds enough of the song to draw all of it (90%, because `bars()` interpolates gaps and holding out for the last few buckets leaves a plain bar on a song whose shape is plainly known), then pins a copy that nothing can change under it; `PlayerWaveform` memoizes its resampled bars off that copy, where the old code re-reduced and re-sorted 512 buckets on every frame of playback. [scar] The store's revision was collected *in composition*, so every recorded sample - ten a second, for the whole of every song - recomposed the player host and all eight styles' progress rows; it is collected in an effect now and read exactly once. The in-memory answer is taken during composition so a song whose shape is already stored has it on the first frame, which is what stops `scrubberTrackHeight` collapsing and re-expanding on every track change. **The entrance is `util/WaveformReveal.kt` and it is pure so the JVM suite can hold it to its endpoints**: a constant-rate sweep across the track with an ease-out-back per bar, each rising from a dot on the centre line and springing past its level. Constant rate rather than a spring because a spring drives the *front* of a stagger and every bar past the front is then clamped, so the bounce lands nowhere; the bounce belongs to each bar. `factorAt` returns exactly 1 for every bar once the sweep is done - a curve that settles at 0.98 would leave the waveform permanently 2% short and quietly wrong about the song, which is not something anybody could catch by looking. The measurement rules themselves are unchanged and were their own scar: **the tap carried a *peak* first and the bar drew as a rectangle on nearly every song**, because peak amplitude in anything mastered sits near full scale almost everywhere. Loudness that visibly differs between a verse and a chorus is RMS. Three smoothers came out with it - buckets averaged within a drawn bar, normalisation against the loudest bucket alone (which only guarantees that *one* bar reaches the top), and a sampler so slow each bucket held a single reading. `bars()` reduces by quadratic mean and contrast-stretches between two percentiles of the song's own distribution; a plain maximum was tried in between and is worse than either, because at roughly four buckets to a bar it picks the local loudest every time and every bar converges. A genuinely level song is drawn as a solid band rather than having dynamics invented from noise. The analyzer measures in 100ms windows to match the sampler's cadence, since the two fill the same buckets for songs that get some of each and a shorter window reports a higher RMS for identical audio. A bucket keeps its loudest visit so a replay cannot erode its own peaks, byte 0 means unmeasured and is distinct from a measured silence. `WaveformStore` is process-wide transport like `VisualizerBus` rather than an eleventh entry on invariant 6's list, and its own preference file is **intentionally absent from `BackupRepository`** - a few hundred envelopes riding along in every backup is exactly the weight a derived cache should not add.

**Both pickers show every style.** Onboarding used to offer a curated pair; that was changed deliberately, because people pick a look at first run and were not finding the rest. `PlayerStylePicker` is non-lazy for exactly this reason: it renders inside onboarding's `verticalScroll` column and inside the settings page's `LazyColumn` item, and a lazy grid nested in a scrollable parent has unbounded height.

**Nine styles, three queue views**, and that is the ratio to keep. `ExpressiveQueueView` (Classic), `GestureQueueView` (Gesture) and `EditorialQueueView` (the other seven) draw genuinely different rows, so the *behaviour* is shared and the visuals are not. `ui/components/QueueReorder.kt` holds the drag state machine and `QueueRowContainer.kt` the lift, swipe-to-remove, undo snackbar and `QueueDragHandle`. **Both live in `ui/components/` rather than `ui/player/` because video mode's `VideoQueueSheet` uses them too**, which is why `queueRowKeys` takes ids rather than `Song`s. A fourth queue view uses them rather than growing a fourth implementation - that is exactly how reordering ended up in one view of three and removal in two.

**Opening any music queue focuses the current song once.** `rememberFocusedQueueListState` waits until the lazy list is laid out, smoothly brings the current row into view and centers it where the list bounds permit; the two queue designs with a now-playing header pass one leading item. It deliberately does not follow later track changes or restart after an interrupted animation, because once the listener scrolls or reorders, the list belongs to them. Closing and reopening the queue creates a fresh state and focuses again.

**Every queue view saves the queue as a local playlist.** [judgement, September 2026] The button sits in the queue header beside the back-to-player control, so all three designs carry it without a fourth list layout, and the write path is shared (`PlayerViewModel.saveQueueAsPlaylist`) rather than per-view. The name dialog is `CreatePlaylistDialog` with a dated pre-fill (`queuePlaylistDateText`, "Queue · 12 Sept 2026"), selected and autofocused so it is overwritten in one gesture. The write is create plus `replacePlaylistSongs`: repeated `addSongToPlaylist` calls would drop duplicate ids, and a queue may legitimately hold the same track twice (`queueTracksForPlaylist` maps occurrences one-to-one and never dedupes - deliberately the opposite of `copyPlaylistToLocal`). It is always local, works signed out, and touches neither playback nor the queue; the confirmation reuses the queue's own removal snackbar through `QueueRemovalController.announce` rather than growing a second host. The action is always offered, even when the queue came from a playlist: provenance is not tracked, and an explicit save that duplicates a playlist is an honest copy the user asked for.

Four rules those files enforce, each something that was wrong before:

- **The visible drag handle is the primary path; long press is only the accelerator.** A queue row is tappable, so `Surface(onClick)`'s clickable sits deeper in the modifier chain than a long-press detector on the same row and wins the gesture; and moving before the timeout hands the pointer to the list's scroll. `LibraryScreen`'s playlist reorder splits the same two ways and only gets away with a row long-press because its clickable is *disabled* in manage mode - a queue cannot do that, since tapping a row must skip to that track.
- **The swap is resolved once per frame from a `withFrameNanos` loop, never in the pointer callback.** `layoutInfo` refreshes only after relayout and several pointer events arrive per frame, so a per-event swap measures against a stale snapshot and the row walks away from the finger.
- **Queue row keys are occurrence-qualified, never index-qualified.** A queue can hold the same track twice so a bare id crashes on duplicate keys, but `"$id_$index"` re-keys every row a moved row passed and `animateItem` then has nothing to animate.
- **Auto-scroll only when the list can consume it.** A `scrollBy` the list cannot take is passed on through nested scroll, and the video queue lives in a `ModalBottomSheet` - unguarded, dragging a row against the top drags the sheet closed mid-reorder.

Music writes the session once on release (`moveQueueItem(..., persist = false)` per crossing, then `commitQueueOrder()`); per-crossing writes turned the gesture into stutter.

**Video mode's queue is edited through `VideoQueue` itself, not the ViewModel.** `moved`, `removedAt`, `withInserted` live on the data class because `index` points at the playing video and every edit must keep it there - a reorder crossing the current index shifts it, a removal above it decrements it - and there is no id to fall back on. Two removals are refused outright, with no control offered rather than one that fails: the last remaining entry, and **the entry that is playing**, because video mode has no playback service to hand off to and the player would go on playing a video the queue no longer contains. `enqueueVideo` starts an ad-hoc queue (`VideoQueue.adHoc`, `playlistId` null) from whatever is already playing, which is what makes "add to queue" work from a feed or search. `moveQueueItem` and `removeQueueItem` check `timelineAgreesAt` before touching the player, as `skipToQueueItem` always has: the UI queue and the ExoPlayer timeline can drift, and editing the wrong index is worse than editing none.

**Every style has an overflow button, and it opens the one menu.** `NowPlayingOptionsSheet` (`ui/player/NowPlayingOptionsSheet.kt`) is start-a-radio, add to playlist, like, download, go to artist, go to album, share, stop-recommending, and the speed and volume controls for the playing song.

**That sheet is a control panel, not a list.** [judgement September 2026] Three bands, in the order a thumb reaches them: four **tiles** across the top (radio, playlist, like, download), a wrapping row of **pills** for the places it can leave to (the artist, the album, share, don't-recommend), and the two **sliders** last. Seven 56dp rows spent the whole sheet on labels for actions whose icons already said what they were, gave a destination the same line as an action, and left the one thing here that is *dragged* wherever it fell in the stack. Like and Download carry their state in the tile's own container rather than a trailing tick, so what is already done is legible across the sheet; the tile squares off as it fills, the same selection language as the app's connected `ToggleButton` groups. A name cannot be a one-word tile label, which is why the destinations are pills - "Go to Kendrick Lamar" and an album title are as long as they are, and pills wrap to as many lines as they need. The long-press `SongOptionsSheet` **stays a list** on purpose: it acts on an arbitrary row in a screen, where a name and a subtitle are the point. Both still draw their primitives from `PlayerOptionRows.kt` (`OptionRow`, `OptionTile`, `OptionPill`), so the vocabulary is shared even where the shape is not.

**That sheet wears whatever the player behind it wears - and a surface gets one palette whole, never a mix.** [judgement October 2026, reversing September's] The expanded player optionally re-themes its accents from the cover (`rememberArtworkColorScheme`), and a `ModalBottomSheet` is a subcomposition, so the menu and the playlist picker it opens inherit that scheme: the app's palette, or with album colours on, the app's surfaces with accents from the cover. For a few weeks it was forced back to the app's palette (`MaterialTheme(colorScheme = appColorScheme())`) on the reasoning that a menu of actions is app furniture; in use it read as a menu belonging to a different screen from the player it slid up over, whose controls were all album-coloured, and testers reported it as an inconsistency. **The half of that decision that survives is the rule about mixing.** The artwork scheme replaces the accent roles and keeps the app's surfaces, so picking roles out of both schemes one at a time gives a palette that is neither. Tinting the surfaces toward the cover was tried and is worse. `LocalAppColorScheme` (read through `appColorScheme()`) still exists for a surface inside the player that genuinely belongs to the app; it takes that scheme whole.

**Speed and volume sit at the foot, as one deck.** They are the only two controls in the sheet and the only two things dragged in it, so they belong under the thumb that opened it rather than above a row of buttons the hand reaches across - which is a reversal: the speed slider used to lead the sheet, and that was right while it was one control in a stack of list rows. One `ControlRow` scaffold draws both, so the label column, the reserved value width and the slider inset are the same measurements in each; mute lives in the trailing slot where speed keeps its reset, rather than on the leading icon, because that keeps the two rows' geometry identical. Speed applies as it moves and persists on release (`setPlaybackSpeed(..., persist = false)` during the drag), because speed is judged by ear but is one decision rather than forty preference writes.

**The volume slider moves the device's media stream, and must not be anything else.** `util/MediaVolume.kt` owns it. `player.volume` in `MusicService` already has two jobs - the loudness correction and the crossfade/duck curve - and every write is `trackGain * curve * duckGain` rather than a bare 1.0, so an app-level gain multiplied onto that field would be a fourth writer of one field and the first thing to break a fade. `STREAM_MUSIC` is also what the hardware keys move and what the video player's vertical drag moves, so the three controls cannot disagree, and nothing is persisted because the stream level already survives the process on its own. The slider snaps to the stream's own steps (15 on most phones, sometimes 25 or 100) for the same reason the speed slider snaps to 5%: a continuous thumb would name a level that is not audible, and the tick on each crossing is the cadence the keys have. The level can move without us - keys, a headset, the system panel - so `rememberMediaVolume` follows a `ContentObserver` on the system settings table plus `ON_RESUME`; neither is load-bearing, because every write reads the stream first, and a missed signal costs a stale thumb rather than a wrong write. [judgement] There is no public broadcast for a volume change (`android.media.VOLUME_CHANGED_ACTION` is unexported), and Media3's device-volume commands would mean enabling device volume control on both engines and handing session controllers a volume provider - a far larger change than a stale slider justifies. The row hides itself where `AudioManager.isVolumeFixed` is true, rather than drawing a control that does nothing. Each style draws the button with its own primitive - `EditorialCircleButton`, `MorphUtilityButton`, `BentoTile`, Classic's `FilledIconButton`, Gesture's plain `IconButton`, Hero's `HeroChromeButton` - because the top bar is part of that style's identity, and **it is last in its action row**, which is where an overflow goes. What it replaced was an Add-to-playlist button, and the eight separate `AddToPlaylistSheet` hosts behind it: one action had a permanent slot in every player while radio, artist, album and share had none, seven of the eight never called `loadYouTubePlaylistsForSheet` so their picker silently listed local playlists only, and six passed `localPlaylists` rather than `addToPlaylistItems`. A ninth style adds a button, not a menu. Go to album is device-library only, and deliberately: `Song.album` is a display string, so it resolves to a real album page for a file on this device (`LibraryContent.initialAlbum`, the same hand-off shape as `initialArtist`) and to nothing at all for a YouTube song. Share is the mirror image - a device file has no link to send. Both are hidden rather than disabled.

**The two option sheets share their rows, not their contents.** `ui/player/PlayerOptionRows.kt` holds `OptionGroup`, `OptionRow`, `OptionRowDivider` and the song header used by both `SongOptionsSheet` (long press) and `NowPlayingOptionsSheet` (the player's overflow), for the reason `QueueReorder.kt` is one file: two menus reached by different gestures must not drift into two different-looking menus. **Both bodies scroll**, and that is load-bearing rather than tidy - a bottom sheet whose content is a plain `Column` clips everything past the sheet's height with nothing logged, identically at every data size, which is how `VideoOptionsSheet` shipped with three unreachable rows. Rows are grouped into shared containers rather than stacked as standalone cards, because six 76dp cards is what overflows in the first place.

**`addToQueue` had no caller for a long time** except the auto-queue, so adding one track to what was playing was impossible. `ui/player/SongOptionsSheet.kt` is the entry point now - Play next, Add to queue, Add to playlist, Like, Download, Go to artist - opened by a long press through `Modifier.songRowClick` and hosted **once** in `HomeScreen` (`songOptionsTarget`), because it acts on the `PlayerViewModel` that lives at that level. Screens take `onSongLongPress: ((Song) -> Unit)?` and pass it down rather than hosting their own. [scar] `onArtistClick` is nullable and that single host was the only call site, so for as long as the row existed it was never once rendered - a nullable wiring parameter defaulting to null is the per-call-site wiring trap in `screens.md`, and one host does not make it safe.

**Swipe up on the expanded player opens `NowPlayingOptionsSheet`.** [judgement September 2026] The gesture lives beside swipe-down-to-collapse on `ExpandablePlayer`'s container, so it works in every style without per-style gesture code, and children that consume vertical drags (queue lists, lyrics, the dial, the sticker) keep them. Its threshold is 80dp against collapse's 50px, so a slightly upward flick does nothing. Each style's `showOptions` is `rememberNowPlayingOptionsOpen()`, hoisted through `LocalNowPlayingOptionsOpen`; a new style must use it or the swipe will open nothing. Collapsing the player closes the sheet.

**Queue lessons that generalise.** [scar] `rememberSwipeToDismissBoxState` keeps the first `confirmValueChange` it was given, so a callback closing over a row index removes the wrong song once anything above it moves; read it through `rememberUpdatedState` (the same hazard applies to any remembered state holder taking a lambda). A queue row is tappable, so a row long-press loses the gesture to `Surface(onClick)` and to list scroll; the visible drag handle is the primary path and long press the accelerator. A ViewModel method with no caller is not a feature - `addToQueue` existed for a long time reachable only from auto-queue.

**Swipe-to-skip exceptions are deliberate.** [judgement] Sticker keeps its peel (the art is thrown off and the next slapped on); Gesture's title swipe steps its carousel through the queue rather than the ViewModel skip, so the carousel following `currentIndex` cannot disagree; the mini player's skip is its own shape - the pill stays put and the song inside it slides, with the neighbour in play order peeking in (`MiniSkipCarousel`, see `playback-video.md`) - and it jumps to that exact occurrence with `skipToQueueItem(id)` rather than sending "previous", which past three seconds restarts the song. Down dismisses the pill; sideways stopped doing so in September 2026.

**The mini carousel holds the neighbouring item, not the code that draws it.** [scar, October 2026] While a committed swipe waits for the bar's own content to become the song that slid in, the carousel keeps drawing the neighbour as it was before the commit. It first did that by holding the neighbour's composable lambda, which froze nothing: Compose remembers one lambda object per call site and swaps its body in place on recomposition, so the held reference drew the caller's newest neighbour the moment the skip landed - the song after the new one, in the middle of the bar, until the hand-off. `MiniSkipCarousel` therefore takes `previous` and `next` as data plus one `neighbour` slot, and holds the data from the last composition before the commit (a swipe dragged the whole way finishes its slide in a frame, so the first composition that sees the commit can already be too late). A commit's timeout only ends its own commit, so a second swipe inside it is not sprung back.

**Album colours can be the whole app.** [judgement October 2026] The setting is Off / Player / Whole app, stored as the old switch plus `artwork_colors_whole_app` (default true), so an install that had it off stays off. Whole app, in music mode, passes the playing cover's main colour to `IvorMusicTheme(artworkSeed)`, which builds the complete scheme with `buildSeedColorScheme` - surfaces included, in the user's palette style, AMOLED applied - through the generator the preset palettes use. Video mode, nothing playing or no artwork is the chosen palette. `rememberFadedColorScheme` fades only when the seed changed (500 ms, each frame a new scheme and so a recomposition of everything reading a colour); a theme or palette change still lands at once. The rule that a surface takes one scheme whole is kept by `LocalAlbumTheming`: with Whole app on, `rememberArtworkColorScheme` returns a complete scheme from *that surface's* cover, so a playlist page is its own artwork while the app round it is the playing album's, and the player is the same scheme as the app. Widgets stay on the palette (`kodaColorScheme`).

**The mini player's cover is a turning, morphing shape and its own progress ring.** [judgement October 2026] `MiniPlayingArtwork` spins the cover (36 degrees a second) and walks it through five Material shapes, holding each, while the song plays; paused, it holds where it is. Three scars from getting there. A round ring hid the morph, so the progress is the shape's outline. Trimming that outline's path to the played fraction made the fill leap at every shape change, because a path starts wherever its polygon begins and that point turned with the cover; the outline is now drawn whole and the played colour is whatever lies inside a fixed wedge opening clockwise from twelve o'clock. And a Material shape is normalised to its square as drawn, not as it turns, so the clover and the soft square swung their corners out of the slot; every shape is fitted to the inscribed circle (`fittedToCircle`, from `calculateMaxBounds`). The angle and shape clock are written from a frame loop and read only in `graphicsLayer` and draw blocks.

**The music pill is a colour, and it is its own progress bar.** [judgement October 2026] The collapsed pill is `primaryContainer` (the album's, with album colours on) rather than a container grey, so it never competes with the page tone it floats over; it hands over to the player's surface in the first 30% of the expansion. It is 68dp, the bubble's size, so closing into the bubble only changes the width. Play is the one filled control; next is a plain icon. `MiniProgressFill` tints the played part with the accent behind the title, its leading edge a sine that travels while playing and eases flat on pause. Progress for the fill and the cover's outline comes from `rememberSmoothProgress`, read through a lambda in draw blocks: the 1Hz sample ticked visibly across a pill-wide fill. Tried and dropped: a blurred cover behind the pill (muddy, and text contrast depended on the cover) and joining the pill to the floating toolbar as one block (the toolbar leaves on the first pixel of scroll, before the pill latches, so the block came apart with its squared corners showing). The pill has no drop shadow.

**The pill latches into its bubble; it does not track the toolbar.** [scar October 2026] Tied to the toolbar's hidden fraction it opened and closed on every small correction, because the toolbar returns on the first pixel of an upward scroll. `HomeScreen` latches it from the toolbar state's `contentOffset`: closed after `MINI_BUBBLE_COLLAPSE_SCROLL` down, open after `MINI_BUBBLE_EXPAND_SCROLL` up or as soon as the list is back where the downward run began (which is what opens it at the top of a list), reset on a tab change. The bubble is the pill's own artwork slot: the surface closes from the start side in a `layout` modifier and the content is placed start-aligned, so the same cover rides into the circle.

**The mini player is customisable, and its defaults are the pill as it was.** [judgement October 2026] `MiniPlayerCustomization` (one snapshot in `ThemePreferences`) holds up to three buttons in the order they were picked, what a tap on the cover does, how the cover moves, the line under the title, what touch and hold does, the two swipes, the colour and when it shrinks to a bubble. `MiniPlayerContent` takes the snapshot and draws from it; `ExpandablePlayer` paints the container and gates the swipes from the same snapshot; Home reads only `shrink`, because it owns the bubble latch and the bars that move with it. Three things to keep: the pill's content colour follows its container (accent or neutral) through one `contentColor`, never a role named directly; a cover motion switched off returns to rest rather than freezing mid-turn; and with no play button chosen a resolving song still shows the loading indicator on its own. A queue button was left out on purpose: each of the nine styles opens its queue from local state, so opening it from the pill is part of the full player work in #317. Touch and hold opening the options sheet works because that sheet's open flag is already shared above the styles (`LocalNowPlayingOptionsOpen`).
