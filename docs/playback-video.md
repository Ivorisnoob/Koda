# Video playback (`ui/video/VideoPlayerViewModel.kt`), SponsorBlock, live, Shorts

Reference detail moved out of `CLAUDE.md` so it loads only when needed. `CLAUDE.md` keeps the summary, the invariants and the silent-failure index; this file keeps the reasoning and the scars. Markers (`[verified]`, `[scar]`, `[judgement]`, `[drifts]`) mean what `CLAUDE.md` says they mean. If a change contradicts a sentence here, fix the sentence in the same commit.

**Video watch progress is separate from resume eligibility.** [verified September 2026] `VideoWatchTracker` owns one video/Short playback: 10s/5s of actual playing wall time qualifies history, local progress checkpoints every 15s, and authenticated WEB watch-time reports every 30s plus pause/seek/close. `VideoItem.watchedProgress` persists inside the existing profile-scoped history JSON, including completed videos; old resume checkpoints supply a migration fallback. The shared video thumbnail uses the newer local/account snapshot. WEB FEhistory supplies `contentImage.thumbnailViewModel.overlays[].thumbnailBottomOverlayViewModel.progressBar.thumbnailOverlayProgressBarViewModel.startPercent`; the signed-in VOD probe reported 60s and read back 10%. `/player` supplies both playback and watchtime URLs, reused under one cpn, and seek discontinuities close the old interval instead of claiming the skipped span. History-off, incognito and profile/cookie identity are rechecked before remote writes. Device/external ids never reach YouTube; live streams have no percentage. Reporting is best-effort while playback is alive, with no persistent offline upload queue. Music history is separate and unchanged.

**A signed-out history write looks exactly like a working one.** [verified September 2026] A session YouTube no longer accepts still gets `/player` `status: OK`, both tracking URLs and a 204 on every ping, on WEB and WEB_REMIX alike, and the play lands in neither FEhistory nor FEmusic_history. The only tell is `logged_in: 0` in the `/player` responseContext, so `historyPlayerSignedOut` gates both reporters on it, skips the pings and notes the expired badge. Cookie shape is not the variable: the same account recorded from the full jar, the WebView login's first capture, and the five bare `SID/HSID/SSID/APISID/SAPISID` cookies. Every early return on both paths logs its reason, because a user report of history "not syncing" (#287) arrived with an empty log and the account's reads working.

**Video and Shorts players are lazy.** Their ViewModels still exist at Home scope because overlays, route hand-offs and restored sessions address them from several tabs, but an ExoPlayer is not built until a video is opened or a saved video session is restored. Closing video cancels its 500ms progress poll. Shorts resolves two upcoming stream ladders rather than five and warms actual progressive bytes only for the immediate next item (smaller on metered networks); live/adaptive sources are never range-warmed. MusicService no longer downloads recommendations and playlists merely because the app bound its controller—Android Auto browse performs its existing cold fetch or stale-while-revalidate when a client actually asks.

**Video loads in two phases** (`VideoPlayerViewModel.startVideo`, which `playVideo` and the queue both funnel into). Phase 1 calls `getVideoStreamQualities()` - stream URLs only, 15s timeout - and starts playback, deliberately through one NewPipe `fetchPage()`. Phase 2 runs `getWatchNextData()` in parallel: a single `/next` parsed into engagement, enriched metadata and related videos. Do not add another per-video extraction or extra `/next` calls there. The video ExoPlayer uses a tuned `DefaultLoadControl`, as `MusicService` does; see the seek-latency paragraph in `playback-streams.md`, because those numbers decide how a scrub feels rather than only how playback starts.

**Video's mini-to-expanded is the music player's container transform.** [scar] One rounded container grows out of the collapsed bar into the window: the bar's own content fades out over the first 28%, the watch page - measured once at window size - rides the container's top edge and is solid by the halfway point, and it settles on the music player's spring. The frame (`playerContainerFrame`), the fade curves and the spring live in `ui/components/PlayerContainerTransform.kt` and `ExpandablePlayer` reads the same ones, so the two players open and close alike rather than drifting apart as copies; the resting states are the frame's endpoints, not separate layouts, and `PlayerContainerTransformTest` checks it lands on the bar and on the window, never shrinks while opening, and that the two layers overlap rather than leaving an empty card between them. Under the transform a drag moves the page's top edge 1:1 with the finger (`dragRangePx` is the travel from the window top to the bar's), so a pull down shrinks the page into the bar the whole way. **This is the fourth design, and the history is the reason for it.** A transform-and-fade of the whole player was replaced by a reveal curtain to avoid promoting the full-screen player on low-end GPUs; the curtain covered the fact that nothing moved (a drag down dimmed the screen by 16% and the video sat still until release); then a shared element flew the picture alone into the bar's 92dp frame and opened the page around it at the end. That one landed correctly but was a different animation from the music player's beside it, which is why both now share one. The cost the curtain dodged is real here for the few frames the page fades (0.12-0.5 of the progress): a full-window layer with alpha is composited offscreen, so a device that stutters on open is the first thing to suspect.

**Which means the portrait player is a TextureView now** (`portrait_video_surface.xml`), for the reason the mini bar already was: a SurfaceView is composited in its own layer behind the app window and follows nothing Compose does to its ancestors - not a rounded clip, not alpha, not a `graphicsLayer` scale. [verified September 2026 against developer.android.com/media/media3/ui/surface] The platform's own advice that SurfaceView animates fine from API 24 is about *view* animation; it does not make one follow a Compose layer. **Two layouts keep the SurfaceView and therefore keep the curtain**: an HDR rendition, which needs it for output, and the full-bleed vertical live player. Both are decided from state that cannot change without re-preparing playback - the rendition, and live-and-portrait - because the host must not change mid-animation: moving the page between parents disposes the video view and re-creates it, which is a black frame in the middle of the animation meant to hide exactly that. `supportsAnimatedMinimize` is the one predicate, read by both the page choosing its surface and the overlay choosing its transition.

**Nothing in that transition may read progress during composition.** The frame is resolved inside `offset {}`, `layout {}` and `graphicsLayer {}` only; a composition read would recompose the whole watch page, and its Android video view, on every frame of the gesture. The one composition read is whether the bar's content is composed at all - a `derivedStateOf` boolean inside its own small composable (`ContainerMiniLayer`), so it flips twice per transition and recomposes nothing else. The page is measured once at `requiredSize` of the window inside an unbounded wrapper, so the container's changing constraints never reach it - the arrangement the music player already used so its content does not reflow with the pill. **Only one view may hold the player's surface, and the container is the resting bar itself.** [scar] The bar's frame takes the surface the moment its content starts fading in on the way down (`barHoldsSurface`: a committed collapse or a finger below that point), the page holds it the rest of the time and from the first frame of an expansion, and the view letting go keeps its last frame instead of closing `PlayerView`'s black shutter (`bindVideoSurface`, `keepContentOnPlayerReset`). The first version drew the video's thumbnail in the bar's frame while the page held the picture and handed over to a separate resting bar at rest, which read as the thumbnail flashing up for a split second before the video came back; handing over to a fresh view at rest would flash its empty first frames instead, so for every session it can carry the transform rests as the bar, gestures included (`miniBarGestures`), and the curtain's bar is left to HDR and vertical live. The hand-off needs no ordering between the two views, because ExoPlayer clears a TextureView only if it is the one being rendered into [verified September 2026 against the Media3 1.11.0 bytecode]. **The transition answers with one small tick when it lands**, open or closed (`KodaHaptics.subtle` after the settle, skipped when nothing moved so a restored player does not buzz on launch); the bar's swipe up no longer ticks at its threshold or on release, while the dismiss swipe keeps both, being a different action. **The two containers differ in one way.** Music's holds opaque until its content is solid and then fades to transparent (`containerBackdropAlpha`), because fading both together showed Home through the middle of the expansion; video's stays opaque throughout, because the watch page draws no background of its own and stands on it.

**A video opened from a playlist carries the playlist with it** (`data/VideoQueue.kt`). [scar] Without one, tapping the third entry played that video and then auto-played the first *related* video - a recommendation, almost never the fourth entry. `playQueue` is the only thing that establishes a queue; `playVideo` clears it, because a related video, a channel row or a search result all mean "done with that playlist", and `startVideo` is the shared private worker so retrying a failed video does not count as leaving. **The queue is addressed by index, never by `videoId`** - a playlist may list the same video twice, which is also why every jump forces a restart rather than letting an id check swallow it. It is a snapshot at tap time, because `HomeViewModel.playlistVideos` is overwritten by the next playlist opened. On `STATE_ENDED` the queue beats the related video outright and advances **even in PiP**. An unplayable entry is stepped over after both retry budgets are spent, capped at `MAX_QUEUE_ERROR_SKIPS` and reset on the first video reaching `STATE_READY`, so one dead entry cannot end the run and a dead network cannot burn the whole playlist. UI: a "Playing from" card, `ui/video/VideoQueueSheet.kt` (hosted by `VideoPlayerContent`, so fullscreen reaches it), next/previous flanking play/pause in both chromes, and a next button on the mini bar. Up Next relabels to "Related videos" while a queue runs.

**Video resume has two deliberately separate stores.** `VideoPlaybackSessionRepository` restores the one player/queue that was active across process death. `VideoHistoryRepository` keeps a bounded, profile-scoped position per replayable video so reopening that video later resumes it even after the player was explicitly closed. The latter checkpoints on switches, backgrounding, close and the existing 15-second persistence tick; it ignores the first ten seconds, clears at the final thirty seconds or 95%, excludes live and one-off external grants, and obeys both incognito and the save-video-history setting. Do not fold it into the active-session snapshot: closing the player must clear that snapshot without discarding the video's reusable progress. **A resume is announced and undoable** (`ui/video/ResumePlaybackChip.kt`, `resumedFromMs`): only a checkpoint raises the notice - a cold session restore already announces itself by arriving paused in the mini player - and "Play from start" drops the stored checkpoint as well as seeking, because being dropped back into the middle on the next open would make the one action the notice offers useless. It sits outside the control-visibility gate for the reason the SponsorBlock chip does, shares that chip's corner, and steps aside while one is showing. The watch page passes `compact`, which drops the leading icon and steps type and padding down again: that chrome draws the video in a band a few hundred dp tall, so a chip sized for fullscreen covers a real fraction of the picture.

**Chromecast was removed in full, and a rebuild is not planned (#132, closed September 2026).** A rebuild, if one is ever reopened, starts from scratch. The old implementation did not work and was carrying `media3-cast`, `play-services-cast` and `androidx-mediarouter` plus a `CastPlayer`/`ExoPlayer` split that reached into both playback pipelines - `VideoPlayerViewModel.activePlayer()`, `MusicService`'s receiver queue, per-style cast buttons, and a receiver-safe quality policy that narrowed the quality sheet. All of it is gone, including the manifest `OPTIONS_PROVIDER_CLASS_NAME` meta-data, so a rebuild starts from a clean sheet rather than inheriting that shape. Two things survive because they were never really about Cast: `deduplicateVideoQualityVariants` still keeps one muxed and one split entry per label, which download selection needs, and `localVideoQualityOptions` (now `ui/video/VideoQualitySelection.kt`) still projects one playback entry per visible label. Do not reintroduce a `Player`-typed indirection for the local player alone - `_exoPlayer` is the only player there is now, and the old alias is exactly what made cast-only no-ops hard to see.

**Player extras.** **Captions** are fetched and parsed independently into `VttCue`s, then drawn by Koda's overlay instead of being merged into the local Media3 source. Switching captions therefore never re-prepares playback or discards the sample buffer; malformed tracks fail to an empty overlay rather than a source error. **Every `PlayerView` Koda draws captions over must call `disableBuiltInSubtitles()`.** [scar] `PlayerView` renders cues into its own `SubtitleView`, and because YouTube captions never reach the Media3 source that view had nothing to draw - so the duplication stayed invisible until device videos arrived. A file's *embedded* subtitles are real cues on the player, and both renderers then drew the same line at once, Koda's in the user's chosen size, colour and background and PlayerView's in the platform's caption style. **Media3 is pinned as one version catalog family** (currently 1.11.0); `LiveUpdateMediaNotificationProvider` delegates the library's notification-channel metadata as well as notification creation so custom progress updates do not fork the channel contract. **Chapters** render as seek-bar ticks, a current-chapter chip and a sheet. **Hold-to-2x** is a long-press in `PlayerGestureSurface` restoring the prior speed on release. **Fullscreen zoom-to-fill** is a pinch open/closed (`RESIZE_MODE_ZOOM`/`FIT`) plus a "Zoom to fill" switch in playback settings, confirmed by a transient tonal pill and reset per video; portrait sources in a landscape window keep their letterbox under the shared `MAX_ACCEPTABLE_CROP` threshold (`zoomFillCropFraction`, tested). **The exit swipe has its own top guard.** [scar, September 2026] The brightness and volume lanes only arm below the top 30% (the notification shade), and the centre lane used to share that line; every swipe out on the emulator began between 21% and 28% of the height and was refused, so "swiping down no longer leaves fullscreen". The centre lane now only keeps clear of the shade's strip (`EXIT_LANE_TOP_GUARD`, 64dp) and is never a level lane. **Share** fires `ACTION_SEND` with `youtube.com/watch?v=<id>`; playlists share `youtube.com/playlist?list=` or the music equivalent, albums `/browse/`, and it is hidden for local playlists and the private WL/LL feeds.

**Sleep timer.** [judgement September 2026] Video mode reuses music's `SleepTimerSheet` (with video wording) from a row in playback settings: duration presets pause playback on a wall-clock deadline and leave the player open, end-of-video is consumed in `handlePlaybackEnded` ahead of autoplay, the queue and related. State is in-memory in `VideoPlayerViewModel` and dies with `closePlayer`, unlike music's service-persisted timer. No fade on expiry - video pauses without one, so the sheet's duration copy says so rather than reusing music's fade wording.

**Smooth motion is video frame interpolation, opt-in behind a warning** (`service/FrameInterpolation.kt`, `service/FrameInterpolationShaderProgram.kt`). [verified September 2026 against the Media3 1.11.0 bytecode; not yet validated on devices] A `GlEffect` in ExoPlayer's `setVideoEffects` pipeline puts frames on a steady output clock (`OutputClock`) at the screen's rate - the lower of the user's cap (Up to 60 / Up to 120, stored as those ints) and the display's fastest mode, so 60, 90 or 120 - and draws each tick between two decoded frames at its phase. Five facts from the bytecode shape it. **The screen follows the output**: `DefaultVideoSink` runs its own `FixedFrameRateEstimator` on the *output* timestamps, and its synced rate is what `VideoFrameReleaseHelper` passes to `Surface.setFrameRate` and paces vsync alignment by (the graph's declared output rate is only the input format's, used until the estimator syncs). A steady 120 fps output asks the display for 120 Hz by itself; an irregular one would never sync, which is why the output is a clock rather than "midpoints plus every real frame" - 25 into 120 has no real frame on most ticks. The step snaps to a whole division of the source interval when one is within 1.5% (30 into 120, 24 into 120), so every Nth output is the decoded frame untouched, and only a change beyond 3% (a new rate or speed) restarts the clock, so a variable-rate device video does not hitch on every wobble. The clock runs in real time (step = speed / fps) and halves its rate rather than draw more than eight frames per decoded one at slow speeds; frames above ~2.2 MP step down to 90 then 60 fps (`affordableFps`, never below 60). A screen that stays below the output for five seconds of steady output (a 60 Hz lock, smooth display off, a non-seamless mode switch) limits the output to what it shows for that video and quality (`ScreenRateWatch`). **The effect graph is chosen once per player**: `MediaCodecVideoRenderer.onEnabled` sets `hasSetVideoSink` on its first enable, so effects must be set before the first prepare and cannot be added later. That is why `ensureLocalPlayer` installs it from a fresh preference read, `closePlayer` rebuilds the player when the setting no longer matches (the one moment nothing holds it), and every per-video decision is `FrameInterpolationControl.active`, read per frame and set from the progress poll by `FrameInterpolationGovernor`. **Any number of outputs per input is legal**: `PlaybackVideoGraphWrapper` hands each output timestamp to the sink on its own and the final stage accepts every frame it is given. **The context is ES 3 or ES 2**, so the shaders are GLSL ES 1.00. **The final stage holds each output until its display time** (in playback it acknowledges input at once and releases each texture only when the sink renders or drops it), so the output pool bounds how far decoding runs ahead: `poolCapacity` keeps about three decoded frames' worth of outputs, within 100 MB, and never fewer than one input's outputs plus two. Motion is estimated once per pair, symmetrically at the midpoint (A(p - d) against B(p + d)), over a three-level luma pyramid with a 320-texel long side, so its cost is independent of resolution. Matching is a zero-mean 3x3 difference plus a quarter of the brightness offset (fades and flashes still match), each level also tries the previous pair's vector (steadier fields, less shimmer), the finest field goes through a 3x3 *vector* median (the neighbour vector with the least summed squared distance to the rest, FidelityFX's optical-flow filter; a per-component median invents vectors none of the neighbours had and broke thin objects) that also writes a per-texel distrust (high cost, or neighbours that mostly disagree with the median - debris, smoke, water), and a 1x1 pass counts unmatched coarse blocks to recognise a scene cut on the GPU. Each drawn tick costs one full-size compose pass that decides **per pixel**: it tries the projected vector and the four field texels around it, keeps the one whose two samples agree best (edges follow pixels, not the 6-pixel grid), fades through a bilinear mask to a plain blend of the two real frames where the field is distrusted, and shows the nearer real frame on a cut. [verified September 2026] Scored as PSNR of the drawn t=0.5 frame against the real frame between two frames a pair apart, over three Tears of Steel clips (camera move past a pole, face and robot arm, explosion): original engine 28.3 dB, repeat 27.2, blend 30.0, this engine 30.8. Measured and rejected: snapping the distrusted areas to the nearer frame (-0.8 dB), leaning on the nearer warped sample where a pixel's pair disagrees (-0.1), and a "detail" field refined on near-full-resolution luma for thin objects (-0.6, and visible speckle even when gated on line-shaped support) - the vector median is what keeps poles and bars intact. [scar, September 2026] The first engine faded to a repeated frame wherever a *block's* residual rose and had no outlier filter, which on device showed as blocky patches with outlined edges, worst at 480p and fast motion; the per-pixel choice, the median and the distrust mask are the fix. `GlProgram` throws `NullPointerException` (not a GL error) for a uniform a driver optimised away, so every interpolation draw is caught as `Exception` and turns interpolation off rather than failing playback. **Two engines, the strongest the GPU runs** (`MotionEngine`). [judgement September 2026] Smooth motion is off by default behind a warning, so only people with capable phones turn it on, and the engine does not hold back for older ones: on a GLES 3.1+ context (Media3 asks for ES 3 and drivers hand back their highest, 3.2 on current phones) it runs `ComputeMotionEngine`; anywhere else, or if that engine fails to build or throws at run time, `FragmentMotionEngine` (the GLSL ES 1.00 engine described above); only when both fail does interpolation stop. The compute engine is FSR3's optical-flow method adapted to video: a luma pyramid from full resolution (capped at a 1920 long side), 8x8 block matching at every level with an exhaustive shared-memory search (+-8 px on coarse levels; +-8 at full resolution up to 720p, +-4 above) seeded by the parent block and its neighbours, the previous pair and zero, with a parabolic sub-pixel fit; **both directions** (A to B and B to A), vector-median filtered and cross-checked into a per-vector confidence (content visible in both frames points back; content being covered or revealed does not); then per drawn tick each vector is **splatted** to where its content is at that moment with a buffer-atomic max on (confidence, speed, index), so the content visible in both frames wins a collision, and resolved into a vector plus how visible its content is in A and in B, so covered content comes from A only and revealed content from B only. [verified September 2026] On the scoring clips it measured 33.5 dB against the fragment engine's 30.8 at t=0.5, and 30.0 against 27.5 across t=0.25/0.5/0.75 with pairs four frames apart (4x motion); the camera move past a pole gained 5-6 dB. Knobs (smoothness weight, occlusion cost, edge bias, trust thresholds) all moved the score by 0.1 dB or less, so the defaults are principled rather than tuned. Two ES 3.1 traps, caught by `glslangValidator` before any device saw them: `imageAtomicMax` needs ES 3.2 or `GL_OES_shader_image_atomic`, so splats go to shader storage buffers, whose atomics are core in 3.1; and `packed` is a reserved word. Rgba16f/rgba8 images are write-only and on immutable (`glTexStorage2D`) textures, which image binding requires. [scar, September 2026] Scene cuts: the first compute build judged a cut only by the share of coarse blocks matching badly (54% at a real cut, against a 50% line), so a cut on the emulator slipped under it and the occlusion path - which trusts a one-sided vector at a fixed cost - assembled a collage of both shots. The cut pass now also counts full-resolution vectors with confidence under 0.25 (99% at a cut, at most 8% on ordinary footage including the explosion at double motion), which also covers pairs drawn far apart when a slow device drops frames.

**Running the compute engine on the emulator.** [verified September 2026, emulator 37.1.11, Intel UHD 630] The default host renderer reports ES 3.0 even with `-feature GLESDynamicVersion` (its GL translator stops there on this driver), so the fragment engine runs. `-feature Vulkan -feature GuestUsesAngle` makes the guest use ANGLE and report ES 3.1, but on this host's Vulkan driver `SurfaceTexture.updateTexImage` then fails in Media3's input stage (the emulator warns "Missing capabilities to support YCbCr content") and playback errors before a frame reaches Koda. What works for correctness: `emulator -avd Pixel_9_Pro_API37 -gpu swangle_indirect -feature GLESDynamicVersion -feature Vulkan -feature GuestUsesAngle -no-snapshot-load` - ANGLE on SwiftShader, ES 3.1, log line `Motion engine: compute (GLES 3.1)`. It renders on the CPU, so it drops most frames: use it to see the engine build and draw, never to judge speed. Nothing of that set-up persists: day-to-day launches (`emu`, `emucold`) stay on `-gpu host` with ES 3.0 and the fragment engine, which is the fastest the emulator gets on this machine.

**Quality is judged offline, not on the emulator**: `.probe/interp/fi/` (never committed) holds `harness.py` (fragment engine) and `harness_compute.py` (compute engine, desktop GL 4.3), which pull the shader strings out of the Kotlin sources and run the same passes with moderngl (Python 3.11 venv in `.probe/interp/venv`) on real frames; `score.py` (t=0.5, pairs two frames apart) and `score_phases.py` (t=0.25/0.5/0.75, four apart) print PSNR tables for any number of source variants (`name=path|compute:--r0 8` for the compute harness; `variant.py` makes a copy with a constant changed); `validate.py` checks every compute-engine shader as GLSL ES 3.10. Re-score and re-validate before changing a shader or threshold. Keep the shaders translatable (`texture2D`/`varying`/`gl_FragColor` only, no ES-only builtins) so it keeps working. The references read while designing this (FidelityFX FSR3 optical flow, ffmpeg `vf_minterpolate`) are cloned beside it. When a stream ends between ticks, its last decoded frame is emitted so playback ends on it. It passes frames through for sources already within 15% of the output rate (60 fps on a 60 Hz screen), live, HDR, thermal status moderate or worse, battery saver, and for the rest of a video at that quality once `FrameDropWatch` judges the phone cannot keep up. There is no speed gate any more: the clock is in real time, so a sped-up source is simply closer to the output rate. [scar, September 2026] The first version acted on `onDroppedVideoFrames`, whose batches arrive at fifty drops or whenever the renderer stops: a quality change stopped it, so twelve seconds of 1080p60 (which is never interpolated) landed as one batch the moment the viewer picked 480p and switched the feature off for exactly the stream it was about to work on. The watch now samples counters every poll, counts only polls that drew frames, forgives a six-second grace after a start, quality change, seek or resume, and judges more than 10% dropped over a sustained ten-to-twelve-second window [judgement September 2026: loosened from 3 s / 5% / 6-8 s after an Adreno 613 at 480p was switched off by its own start-up]; a new video or quality, or closing the player, starts over. [scar, September 2026] The ratio is against the frames the effect *emitted*, not `renderedOutputBufferCount`: with effects on, `droppedBufferCount` also counts output frames the sink dropped late, drawn ones included (the sink listener's `onFrameDropped` calls `updateDroppedBufferCounters(0, 1)` [verified September 2026, 1.11.0 bytecode]), while the rendered count is decoder buffers only, so at 4x a 5% output drop read as 20%. Verified on an emulator (September 2026): a 25 fps video drew to 60 fps, and the emulator's GPU fell behind and the watch passed frames through after the grace period, as designed; 90/120 Hz output still needs a device. `adb logcat -s FrameInterpolation` prints source, output, target and screen rates and the counters every ten seconds of playback. **Settings makes it available; the player's panel switches it.** [judgement September 2026] The Settings switch (`video_frame_interpolation`, behind the warning) decides whether the player is built with the effect graph and whether the playback settings panel offers a Smooth motion switch; that switch (`video_frame_interpolation_player_on`, default on, set back to on whenever Settings turns the feature on) is what the governor treats as the user's choice, so it takes effect within a poll without rebuilding the player, and switching it back on gives the video a fresh drop-watch chance. HDR stays withheld while the graph is installed, whatever the panel switch says. The switch's supporting line is a read-out (`FrameInterpolationStatus`): the measured source rate and the output rate, noting when the frame size or the screen held it below the target, or why it is paused. HDR is withheld from the ladder while it is on or while the player still carries the graph, because an HDR rendition through the graph is tone-mapped or not depending on GL extensions. A shader failure turns interpolation off, never playback. **With the graph installed the player reports no video size.** [verified September 2026 against the 1.11.0 bytecode; scar] The renderer hands the graph's size to its video-sink listener, whose `onVideoSizeChanged` is empty (`MediaCodecVideoRenderer$1`), so `Player.videoSize` stays 0x0 and `onVideoSizeChanged` never fires: `PlayerView`'s frame has no ratio, fills the view, and the graph's last stage (`Presentation`, scale-to-fit at the surface's size) letterboxes inside it - which is how pinch-to-fill showed "Zoomed to fill" and changed nothing. Every view that zooms (fullscreen, mini bar, vertical live) calls `keepKnownAspectRatio` (`ui/video/PlayerViewAspect.kt`) with the page's `videoAspectRatio`, after binding the player, since `setPlayer` and every unknown-size event reset the frame; it also fixes a SurfaceView's buffer at the fitted size, so a zoom scales the buffer instead of resizing the surface (a resize makes the graph re-configure, and frames drawn meanwhile came out at the old size - the zoom flicker). The graph also draws a paused frame once, when a surface arrives and before its size does, so a hand-off while paused (mini bar to page, fullscreen to portrait) left a small picture in the bottom-left corner; `redrawPausedFrameThroughGraph` seeks exactly to the current position once the size settles. Media3's `VideoFrameProcessor.REDRAW` is not the fix: it needs a replayable frame cache only the composition player builds, and throws without one. Device videos go through it too, including HDR files, which the program copies at high precision without interpolating. The Shorts, inline-preview and motion-artwork players do not carry it.

## SponsorBlock

Crowd-sourced segment skipping, **video mode only** and off until switched on (`data/SponsorBlock.kt`, `data/SponsorBlockRepository.kt`, `ui/settings/SponsorBlockSettings.kt`, `ui/video/SponsorBlockChip.kt`).

**The video id never leaves the device.** The request carries the first four hex characters of its SHA-256 and the server answers with every video sharing that prefix, which the client filters locally. The plain `?videoID=` form of the same endpoint exists and is easier; using it would hand a third party a complete record of what every user watches. This is also the only third-party service the app contacts at all, which is why the feature is opt-in and the settings page names the host before the switch rather than after it.

**Three states per category, not one switch.** [judgement] A sponsor read is something nearly everyone wants gone; an intro or an off-topic section in a music video is something plenty of people want kept. `SegmentAction` is `SKIP`/`MANUAL`/`IGNORE`, defaulting to skip for sponsor, self-promo and interaction reminders and ignore for the rest - matching SponsorBlock's own defaults. `IGNORE` is enforced at the *request*, not after it: `activeCategories` decides what is asked for, so an ignored category is never received.

**`SponsorCategory.apiName` is the wire format and is frozen**, the same way a persisted enum constant is - the encoded preference stores it, so renaming one resets that category to its default for every existing user. `encodeSegmentActions`/`decodeSegmentActions` are one preference key rather than eight, and tolerate unknown names and unknown actions in both directions so an older and a newer build cannot poison each other's stored value.

**The skip decision rides the existing 500ms progress poll** rather than adding a timer, so a skip can land up to one tick late; submitted segments carry their own padding, and a second loop was not worth frame accuracy. No scrubbing guard is needed because `PlayerSeekBar` commits on release, so the player's position does not move mid-drag. **Overlapping segments resolve to the one ending latest** (`segmentAt`), because a nested self-promo inside a sponsor read would otherwise skip to the nearer end, land back inside the outer segment and skip again - one stutter instead of one skip.

**A skipped segment goes into an ignore set keyed by submission UUID.** Undo seeks back and leaves it there: undoing means the viewer wants to watch that part, so asking again on the next tick is the bug. The set is per video and cleared with the rest of the state, since the UUIDs are unique per video and nothing will read them again.

**The chip is deliberately outside the control-visibility gate.** A skip is the one thing that happens without being asked for, so an undo reachable only when the chrome happens to be up is not reachable at the moment it is needed. **Live videos are excluded** - there is no fixed timeline to key a segment against - and only `actionType: skip` is acted on: `full` would skip to the end of the video, `poi` is a point rather than a range, and `mute` needs volume control this does not implement.

**Read-only, on purpose.** Submitting and voting need a persisted anonymous user id, a marking UI and write endpoints; that is its own feature and belongs in its own issue rather than riding along with skipping.

## Live streams

A live broadcast is not a separate content type. It is a normal video whose `/player` response carries an HLS manifest, and everything follows from that.

**Detection is the formats, not the manifest.** [scar] `streamingData.hlsManifestUrl` is present on live responses and absent on VODs *for ANDROID_VR* [verified August 2026], and that client-specific fact was written down as though it were universal. It is not: [verified September 2026] visionOS and IOS return `hlsManifestUrl` on every ordinary VOD. Gating on the key alone therefore made the visionOS HDR augmentation classify every video as live, return an all-SDR HLS ladder, and reduce the entire HDR feature to a silent no-op - green unit tests included, because they built a `streamingData` without the key. The discriminator is whether the renditions describe whole files: a VOD format declares `contentLength` and an ordinary ranged URL, a live one is a segment endpoint carrying neither. `parseDirectVideoQualities` returns the HLS ladder only when the manifest is present *and* no rendition is a VOD file, and stamps `VideoQuality.isLive`; `VideoPlayerViewModel` reads `_isLive` off the quality list rather than paying for a separate call. [scar] Live is the one case where `adaptiveFormats` progressive URLs are unusable - they are segment endpoints (`live=1`, `noclen=1`), so an unbounded GET returns one ~2s segment and a ranged GET blocks until timeout. That was the "live video opens but never plays" failure.

**A live player is never at the end of its own playlist, and the behind-live readout has to measure from where it actually sits.** [scar] With no `EXT-X-START` and no `HOLD-BACK` in the manifest - which is what YouTube sends - Media3 targets `3 x targetDuration` back from the playlist end [verified September 2026 against 1.11's `HlsMediaSource.getTargetLiveOffsetUs`, which falls through to exactly that, and publishes it on `Timeline.Window.liveConfiguration.targetOffsetMs`]. With YouTube's ~5s segments the live edge is therefore 15-16s short of the window end, and `liveWindowOffsetMs` measured `duration - position` against a flat 10s tolerance - so a stream sitting exactly where its own manifest asks it to sit reported a permanent -0:15 and `LiveEdgeChip` never once said LIVE. That offset is not slack to be forgiven with a bigger tolerance, it *is* the edge: `VideoPlayerViewModel.liveTargetOffsetMs` reads it off the timeline on every `onTimelineChanged` (a playlist reload republishes the timeline, so a broadcast that changes its hold-back mid-stream is followed rather than pinned at its opening value) and the three chromes pass it in. Measured from the edge the number also finally means what a viewer reads it as - how far *they* scrubbed back - instead of that plus a constant. A zero target reduces the arithmetic to the old window-end measurement, which is the honest answer for a source that declares no target at all.

**The quality ladder comes from the manifest the player loaded.** [scar] NewPipe used to resolve first and hand back the HLS URL alone (the direct visionOS path now returns `Auto` plus labels guessed from `adaptiveFormats`, which this still replaces), so the menu showed a lone "Auto (HLS)" although the master playlist declares every rung from 144p up [verified September 2026]. `publishLiveQualityLadder` rebuilds the menu on `onTracksChanged` from the supported video renditions (`liveVideoQualityLadder`: short-edge labels, real dimensions and frame rate per rung), gives the default-quality setting its say the first time a ladder replaces a bare Auto, and carries the viewer's pick across by label on playlist reloads. **The ladder is labels only.** Every rendition lives in the one manifest, so all entries carry the same URL and picking one calls `applyLiveQualityCap` (`setMaxVideoSize` at the rung's real frame, so a vertical "720p" is 720x1280, a frame-rate cap so "1080p" does not land on 1080p60, plus `setForceHighestSupportedBitrate`) rather than swapping the media source - quality changes cost no re-prepare and no rebuffer, which matters more on live than anywhere. The cap clears on the next `playVideo`, because track selection outlives media items and a cap picked on a stream would silently limit the next video.

**Live chat** (`getLiveChatSession` → `pollLiveChat`, `ui/video/LiveChatPanel.kt`). The continuation arrives free with the Phase 2 `/next` (`parseLiveChatContinuation`); polling starts only once chat is on screen. The server's interval is honoured but clamped to `LIVE_CHAT_MIN_WINDOW_MS`/`MAX` (a misparse must not become a hot loop), the backlog caps at `MAX_LIVE_CHAT_MESSAGES` (250, matching YouTube's own `maxItemsToDisplay`), and three consecutive failed polls give up. Sending echoes the accepted message back so it appears without waiting a poll; rejections (slow mode, word filter, ban) surface through `onFailure` so the composer restores the text. Super Chat colors come from YouTube and are the documented exception to the color rule. A finished broadcast exposes its replay chat under the same key; nothing consumes that today.

`LiveChatPanel` has three dresses and the `compact` flag selects only between the first two: default is an opaque `surfaceContainer` sheet over the info area (portrait); `compact = true` is a translucent column docked right of the video (landscape); `LiveChatOverlay` is a *separate* composable in the same file, a read-only ticker on a gradient for the full-bleed vertical player, reusing the message rows but passing `authorColorOverride` because the role tints are not guaranteed to carry contrast against video. Sending, scroll-back and the jump-to-latest pill stay with the full panel.

**Vertical live** is an ordinary broadcast encoded 9:16 - there is no "Shorts live" entity. [verified August 2026: `/shorts/<id>` resolves back to `/watch?v=`, its `/next` carries no `reelPlayerOverlayRenderer`, and five pages of `reel/reel_watch_sequence` returned zero live items.] **Orientation is the only signal**, so `VideoQuality.isPortrait` is derived at parse time from the largest dimensioned format on both paths; reading it from `onVideoSizeChanged` instead is too late and makes the layout snap after the first frame, though that remains the backstop. `isLive && isPortraitVideo` selects `VerticalLivePlayerContent` - full-bleed, chat ticker over video, controls on a timer but top chrome always up, immersive on open, `CloseFullscreen` dropping to the standard watch page and `StayCurrentPortrait` returning, the choice resetting per video. Fill-vs-fit is conditional: zoom-to-fill past `MAX_ACCEPTABLE_CROP` (25%) letterboxes instead, because "vertical" also covers 4:5 and 1:1 streams that would lose their top and bottom. **Landscape is the good case for 9:16**: the space beside the video is chat-shaped, so rotating auto-opens the docked column and `FullscreenPlayerContent`'s `videoEndPadding` slides the video clear (portrait sources only - shrinking a 16:9 video to dodge an overlay sitting on letterbox bars loses more than it gains).

**Portrait fullscreen has its own bottom bar, and the flanking readout was the problem.** `compactChrome` already moved the top-bar actions onto a line of their own; the same window has no width for `0:20 [bar] 0:46` either - two labels plus their gaps take roughly two fifths of a phone's short edge, leaving the track too short to scrub accurately and pushing the total hard against its end. In compact the readout is one `0:20 / 0:46` on a line above a full-width seek bar, sharing that line with the chapter chip or the live pill, and the bar stands further off the bottom edge because fullscreen hides the system bars, so `WindowInsets.navigationBars` reads zero and the row would otherwise sit under the gesture bar. **The viewing controls live in that bar too**, above the readout: landscape has width for them beside the title, portrait does not, and putting them on a row of their own under the title left a stray right-aligned cluster at the far end of a tall window, out of reach of the hand holding it. Anything floating over the frame - captions, the SponsorBlock chip, timed comments - measures its clearance from `FULLSCREEN_COMPACT_BOTTOM_BAR` rather than repeating a literal, so the four cannot drift apart; a row added to or taken out of that bar has to move the constant with it. Landscape keeps the bar it always had.

**Comments are hidden entirely on live videos** - the entry row, the timed-comments button in both chromes, the overlay, and the fetch behind it. Live chat is the conversation; a running broadcast's comment section is usually empty or disabled, and offering both sent people to the dead one. An open comments sheet closes when a live stream starts, since the sheet outlives the video it was opened on.

**A Short says what it is now.** The overlay drew four fields off its `VideoItem` - title, channel, avatar, id - while the Phase-2 `/next` that runs for the like rail had already filled in `description`, `descriptionLinks`, `viewCount`, `uploadedDate` and `subscriberCount` on that same object, so a Short was the one place in Koda where you could not find out when something was posted. The stats line under the title is that data and doubles as the affordance for `ShortsDescriptionSheet`, which is a sheet rather than an expanding overlay because a description runs to any length and its links want a real tap target, neither of which belongs over video. The `MoreVert` in the top bar opens the ordinary `VideoOptionsSheetHost` - Shorts is its sixth surface - and **passes its own dismissal callbacks through `onNotInterestedOverride`/`onBlockChannelOverride`**, because Shorts is the one surface that filters on ingestion: routing the hide through the home feeds would record it correctly and leave the Short sitting on screen. The action rail keeps its own "Not interested" chooser; the overflow cannot live in that rail, which is a column of single-purpose taps the thumb rests on while swiping.

**A live item in the Shorts feed is handed off, not approximated:** `ShortsPlayerViewModel` emits `liveHandoff` and closes itself, and `MainActivity` reopens it in the video player. Emit *before* `close()` - it cancels the very job the emit runs in.

**Shorts pagination belongs to one opening and one profile.** [scar September 2026] An untracked sequence coroutine could append to a reopened player; network failures returned an empty page with no token and permanently exhausted the feed. Opening/closing now cancels and invalidates the old sequence request, failures retain the last usable continuation, and publication checks the profile/login and opening generation. A profile switch preserves already-loaded positional pages and current playback, but drops the old token and seeds the next extension from the new profile. Up to three duplicate/hidden-only pages can be traversed per trigger so the end of the pager does not depend on selecting a nonexistent next page; repeated tokens stop the chain. The next swipe retries a failed extension.

**Shorts read ahead before the next swipe.** [judgement September 2026] Sequence extension starts with six loaded items remaining, giving the next-item URL lookahead time to reach the next page. After a 750 ms settling delay, actual audio/video heads are buffered for the immediate next Short only, and speculative work stands down during a 429 hold, including those whose URLs were already cached; see `playback-streams.md`. Device checks should cover several ordinary swipes after watching each clip briefly, rapid forward/back swipes, crossing a sequence-page boundary, closing/reopening, and preload or Shorts caching disabled. Initial cold playback, adaptive-only sources and swipes faster than the connection can preload may still buffer.

**A refused connection gets network advice, not a Retry that fails the same way.** [judgement September 2026] When a playback error coincides with a connection verdict - HTTP 429 or a `YouTubeRateLimit` hold, a googlevideo 403 that survived the re-resolve under a fresh identity, or an active bot-check verdict (armed only after a remint was refused) - `connectionAdviceFor` turns it into a `ConnectionAdvice`, and `ErrorOverlay` (inline, fullscreen, vertical live) and the Shorts page draw `ConnectionAdviceCard` instead of the message: advice for the network kind (VPN checked first, since its address is the one YouTube sees), a Change network button opening `Settings.Panel.ACTION_INTERNET_CONNECTIVITY`, and Retry. Each ViewModel classifies whatever reaches `_playbackError` rather than each of the ten places that set it, because the verdicts are armed by the resolution that produced the error. Offline and device videos are excluded - "change network" is wrong advice for both. While advice stands, `NetworkChangeWatcher` waits for a new *validated* default network (airplane mode and a Wi-Fi rejoin both produce one) and then drops the old address's verdicts, remints visitorData and retries on its own. The card is compact below 300dp of height for the inline player and scrolls rather than clipping at large font scales.

**A Short flicked past costs nothing.** [judgement September 2026] A cancelled job cannot stop a stream extraction that has started, so a skimmer used to pay a full resolution plus a `/next` for every Short passed. A Short started within `SWIPE_STREAK_MS` (1.5 s) of the previous one, with nothing cached, waits `COLD_RESOLVE_DWELL_MS` (450 ms) under its thumbnail before either request, and the Phase-2 `/next` is a tracked job cancelled on the next swipe. A Short watched even briefly is outside the streak, so ordinary swiping, which is served from the preload, never waits.

## Inline feed previews

Off by default (Content settings). A card starts after at least 60% of it is visible with the list settled, plus 700ms of dwell, and gives up below 40% - two thresholds because one makes a card on the boundary flap on every frame of a slow drag. Resolution starts at 300ms so the picture lands at the dwell mark. **One player and one `previewingId`**, so two playing cards is unrepresentable. Previews start muted, with a per-card unmute that never carries to the next preview. Never on a metered connection, with Data Saver, or during a rate-limit hold; stops on scroll, on leaving the card, on lifecycle pause and when a video or Short opens over the feed - explicitly, because the players are above the NavHost and the feed under them is never paused. The surface sat inside an `AnimatedVisibility` gated on "first frame rendered", which cannot happen without a surface - it is one surface faded by alpha now. And the resolver discarded adaptive renditions, which are all most videos have; adaptive goes to the player's source factory as it does in Shorts. **PiP's three buttons depend on what can follow.** [judgement September 2026] In a playlist: previous, play/pause, next (disabled at either end). A lone video with a related one lined up: back 10 s, play/pause, next - the related video autoplay would pick (`playNextOrRelated`); PiP still never autoplays into it on its own. A five-button row was asked for and is why the setting exists instead. The stored values are frozen. The controller follows the setting through its own `ThemePreferences` flow; `currentPipTransport` reads it fresh.

**Both mini bars answer to one gesture set: up expands, down dismisses, sideways skips inside the bar.** [judgement September 2026, superseding the sideways dismiss both bars briefly shared] The bar never moves sideways: `MiniSkipCarousel` (`ui/components/MiniSkipCarousel.kt`) slides the item inside it, clipped, with the neighbour it would move to peeking in from the edge, and the controls beside it stay put. Past 72dp or a fling it slides the neighbour all the way in, *then* skips, and holds until the bar draws that item (its content key equals the key the neighbour had) so the new item lands exactly where the neighbour was; if nothing changes (a refused skip) it springs back after 2.5 s. [scar] Two things made the first version flash a wrong song after a swipe: the neighbours were recomputed the instant the skip landed, so "next" in the middle of the bar became the song *after* the new one, and the music bar was keyed on the queue occurrence id, which moves a moment before the drawn song. The carousel now freezes its neighbours while committing, and keys are built from what is drawn (the song id; the video id). The video bar's skip passes `expand = false` through `playNextOrRelated` / `playPreviousInQueue` to `startVideo`: a swipe changes what is in the bar, it is not a request to watch. Toward an edge with nothing there it gives a little and stiffens. Video's next is the playlist's next or, with no playlist, the first related video (what `playNextOrRelated` plays); previous exists only in a playlist. The overlay owns the drag (`rememberMiniSkipState()` + `miniSkipGesture`) and the bar's content binds the neighbours (`BindMiniSkip`), so the overlay never recomposes on a related list arriving. The downward pull and the Close button stay (Close was restored on purpose in `990db55`).

**Watch as video brings the rest of the music queue.** [judgement September 2026] The songs after the current one in play order that have a video, capped at 50 (`MUSIC_TO_VIDEO_QUEUE_MAX`), become a `VideoQueue` titled "Your music queue", started at the song's position with `playQueueAt`. The cap is what stops a self-topping radio arriving as an endless playlist; past its end related videos take over as before.

**Watched Shorts stay out of the feed.** Pages pulled by `maybeLoadMore` skip ids in local watch history (`VideoHistoryRepository.isWatched`); the shelf the user tapped into is left as it was.
